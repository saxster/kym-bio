# Misconception research brief: biology for Kym (age 12, Mumbai)

Biology has a distinct cognitive trap that physics does not: **human intuition treats living things as purposeful agents ("plants grow roots *in order to* find water", "bacteria mutate *so that* they can survive antibiotics") and treats invisible chemical transfers as magic labels.**

### Three cross-cutting design rules from the biology education literature:
1. **The Three Intuitive Construals (Coley & Tanner 2012, 2015):** In *CBE—Life Sciences Education* ([Coley & Tanner 2015](https://www.lifescied.org/doi/10.1187/cbe.14-06-0094), $n = 138$ biology majors and non-majors), up to **79% of students** endorsed teleological explanations ("species evolve traits *because they need them*"), **68%** endorsed anthropocentric explanations, and **61%** endorsed essentialist explanations (ignoring variation within a species). Every chapter must unpack "wants/needs/in order to" into physical mechanism + differential survival.
2. **Macroscopic-to-Molecular Blindness (*Lessons from Thin Air*, Harvard-Smithsonian 1997; AAAS Project 2061):** Students cannot easily believe that an invisible gas ($\text{CO}_2$) becomes solid wood, or that solid food leaves your body mainly as exhaled $\text{CO}_2$ gas when you lose weight. Every metabolic chapter must track actual **atoms and mass** (linking to *The Invisible World*, Ch 2).
3. **Corrections fade unless revisited (`:::recall`):** Just as in physics (Winer et al. 2002), students taught that plants respire 24 hours a day revert within months to saying "plants photosynthesise in the day and respire only at night". Each core misconception returns in `:::recall N` blocks in later chapters.

## Ranked Topic Misconceptions, Prevalence & Decisive Evidence

### 1. Living vs. Non-Living, Dormancy & Spontaneous Generation (Ch 0 & Ch 1)
- **Misconceptions:**
  - *"Fire, clouds, or rivers are alive because they move, grow, and consume fuel; a dry seed or unhatched cyst is dead because it does not move or use measurable oxygen."* (Driver et al. 1994; Carey 1985: over **50% of 10–12-year-olds** misclassify either fire/sun/clouds or dormant seeds/spores when pushed on criteria).
  - *"Weevils in a sealed tin of rice or maggots in rotting meat appear by themselves out of the food."* (Spontaneous generation intuition).
- **Decisive evidence:**
  - **Dry *moong* seeds vs. candle flame:** Weigh dry *moong* (mung bean) seeds before and after soaking in water, and compare a sealed jar of sprouting *moong* (which fogs up, warms slightly, and turns limewater milky from cellular respiration while building ordered root/shoot tissue) against a candle flame under a jar (which breaks wax down into $\text{CO}_2$ and $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ and snuffs out, leaving zero cellular structure or hereditary code).
  - **Francesco Redi's 1668 three-jar experiment** (open, sealed, gauze-covered) and **Louis Pasteur's 1859 swan-neck flask**.

### 2. Cells & Scale: Size, Interior & Why Cells Are Small (Ch 1 & Ch 2)
- **Misconceptions:**
  - *"Atoms, molecules, viruses, bacteria, and human cells are all roughly the same 'microscopic' size."* (Tretter et al. 2006; AAAS Project 2061: **54% of middle-schoolers** think molecules are larger than or the same size as cells).
  - *"A cell is a flat 2D fried-egg drawing filled with empty water around a few floating organelles."* (In reality, the cytoplasm is a crowded 3D molecular city—roughly $20\text{–}30\%$ protein/RNA by volume).
  - *"An elephant is bigger than a mouse because an elephant's cells are bigger than a mouse's cells."* (**48% of middle-school students** on AAAS items).
- **Decisive evidence:**
  - **Surface-Area-to-Volume Ratio ($SA/V$) cube calculation & kitchen test:** Cut three cubes of firm *paneer* or raw potato ($0.5\text{ cm}$, $1\text{ cm}$, $2\text{ cm}$ on a side) and soak them in beetroot juice or turmeric water for 15 minutes, then slice them in half. The dye penetrates the same distance ($\approx 2\text{ mm}$) into all three: the $0.5\text{ cm}$ cube is reached right to its core, while the centre of the $2\text{ cm}$ cube is still bone-white and "starved".

### 3. Diffusion, Osmosis & Membranes (Ch 3)
- **Misconceptions:**
  - *"Molecules move from high to low concentration because they 'want' more space or 'try' to reach equilibrium."* (**62% of students**, Odom & Barrow 1995 *Osmosis and Diffusion Conceptual Assessment*).
  - *"Once equilibrium is reached, molecules stop moving."* (**58% of students**, Odom & Barrow 1995).
  - *"In salt water, salt rushes into a plant cell and bursts it / water moves because salt 'sucks' it."*
- **Decisive evidence:**
  - **3D random-walk particle simulation:** Every molecule moves in a blind, random thermal bounce (`:::recall` *The Invisible World* Ch 3). More molecules on the left simply means more random bounces crossing left-to-right than right-to-left every millisecond. At equilibrium, equal numbers cross both ways—zero net change, nonstop motion.
  - **Kitchen Forensics:** Soak two identical raw potato strips (measured to the nearest millimetre, $60\text{ mm}$) for 30 minutes—one in plain tap water (grows to $\approx 63\text{ mm}$, rock-stiff/turgid), one in concentrated salt water (shrinks to $\approx 56\text{ mm}$, limp/flaccid).

### 4. Biological Molecules & Enzymes (Ch 4)
- **Misconceptions:**
  - *"High temperatures 'kill' enzymes."* (Enzymes are single folded protein molecules, not living organisms; they cannot die—thermal jiggling breaks the weak hydrogen/ionic bonds holding their 3D active site in shape, which is **denaturation**).
  - *"Enzymes are used up in the reaction they catalyse."* (**46% of students**, AAAS Project 2061).
- **Decisive evidence:**
  - **Raw vs. boiled potato/liver with $6\%$ pharmacy hydrogen peroxide ($\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$)** or **fresh raw papaya/pineapple juice vs. boiled juice on set *dahi* (curd)/gelatin**: raw catalase froths $\text{O}_2$ violently and can froth a second batch of $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$ (proving the enzyme was not used up); boiled potato produces zero bubbles because catalase is denatured.

### 5. Photosynthesis & Where Plant Mass Comes From (Ch 5)
- **Misconceptions:**
  - *"Plants get their food and almost all of their solid weight from the soil through their roots."* (**85%+ of students**, Harvard *Minds of Our Own* / AAAS Project 2061 item `ME029`: **73% of middle-schoolers** reject the claim that a tree's dry mass comes mainly from carbon dioxide gas in the air).
  - *"Sunlight is a substance/ingredient that gets baked into sugar."* (Light provides energy to split water and lift electrons, not atoms of matter).
- **Decisive evidence:**
  - **Jan Baptista van Helmont's 5-year willow tree experiment (published 1648):** A $2.27\text{ kg}$ ($5\text{ lb}$) willow sapling grew to $76.8\text{ kg}$ ($169\text{ lb } 3\text{ oz}$) in a covered pot of oven-dried soil, while the dry soil lost only $57\text{ g}$ ($2\text{ oz}$)! Combined with Ingenhousz (1779) and Saussure (1804): the carbon atoms that make wood cellulose ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_{10}\text{O}_5$)$_n$ come from invisible $\text{CO}_2$ gas entering microscopic leaf pores (stomata).

### 6. Cellular Respiration & Weight Loss (Ch 6)
- **Misconceptions:**
  - *"Respiration means breathing in and out with your lungs."* (**67% of 12–14-year-olds**, Driver et al. 1994).
  - *"Plants photosynthesise during the day and respire only at night."* (**64% of students**, Haslam & Treagust 1987).
  - *"When you exercise and lose 1 kg of fat, that mass turns into heat/energy or sweat."* (Meerman & Brown 2014 *BMJ*: over **$84\%$ of lost fat mass** is exhaled into the air as $\text{CO}_2$ gas; the remaining $16\%$ becomes $\text{H}_2\text{O}$).
- **Decisive evidence:**
  - Stoichiometric atom-tracking of glucose ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\text{O}_2 \to 6\text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O}$) and fat oxidation, plus fermenting *idli* batter or yeast + sugar in a warm Mumbai kitchen inflating a balloon with $\text{CO}_2$.

### 7. Human Circulation, Digestion & Homeostasis (Ch 7–10)
- **Misconceptions:**
  - *"Deoxygenated blood in your veins is bright blue, like the lines on your wrist or textbook diagrams."* (**52% of students**; venous blood is dark maroon-red, never blue—your skin scatters red and blue light differently).
  - *"Your stomach is a big bag taking up your whole belly, and food goes straight from the stomach into the bloodstream or out as faeces."* (In reality, the small intestine does $>90\%$ of chemical digestion and absorption, and faeces are mostly undigested plant fibre, water, and dead gut bacteria, not metabolic 'excretion').

### 8. DNA, Genes, Mitosis & Inheritance (Ch 11–13)
- **Misconceptions:**
  - *"Only certain cells have DNA (e.g., brain cells have brain DNA, skin cells have skin DNA, and plants have no DNA because they aren't animals)."* (**51% of middle-schoolers** think plants lack DNA or that different body cells carry different DNA; AAAS Project 2061).
  - *"A dominant allele is stronger, healthier, and more common in the population than a recessive allele."* (e.g., polydactyly—having six fingers—is caused by a dominant allele, yet $>99.8\%$ of people have five fingers).
- **Decisive evidence:**
  - **Kitchen DNA extraction** from mashed banana or *papaya* using dish soap, table salt, and chilled surgical spirit (isopropyl alcohol) to spool visible white threads of plant DNA onto a toothpick.

### 9. Natural Selection, Mutations & Antibiotic Resistance (Ch 14)
- **Misconceptions:**
  - *"When exposed to an antibiotic or a new climate, individual organisms mutate or adapt on purpose in order to survive, and pass that acquired change to their offspring."* (**79% of students**, Bishop & Anderson 1990; Gregory 2009).
  - *"Antibiotic resistance means your human body becomes resistant/immune to the antibiotic."* (WHO 2015 multi-country survey: **76% of respondents** believed the human body, rather than the bacteria, becomes resistant to antibiotics!).
- **Decisive evidence:**
  - **Luria & Delbrück (1943) / Lederberg replica plating (1952)** and the Harvard Mega-Plate experiment: mutations happen *randomly before* the bacterium ever meets the antibiotic; the antibiotic simply kills the susceptible majority and leaves the pre-existing mutant with the whole plate to itself.
