## 2. Key Historical Scenes & TRUE / HEDGE / LEGEND Audit

1. **`:::scene Vilvoorde (near Brussels), 1648`** — **TRUE.** Van Helmont dried $200\text{ lb}$ ($90.7\text{ kg}$) of soil in a furnace, planted a $5\text{ lb}$ ($2.27\text{ kg}$) willow shoot, covered the pot with a perforated tin plate so dust could not settle in, and watered it only with rainwater or distilled water for five years. The tree weighed $169\text{ lb } 3\text{ oz}$ ($76.74\text{ kg}$); the re-dried soil weighed $199\text{ lb } 14\text{ oz}$ (losing only $2\text{ oz} \approx 57\text{ g}$). **Important ironic twist (TRUE):** Van Helmont concluded the tree's wood came from *water alone*—because he himself had discovered "gas sylvestre" ($\text{CO}_2$) yet didn't realise leaves were pulling that invisible carbon out of the air!
2. **`:::scene Florence, summer 1668`** — **TRUE.** Francesco Redi placed snake meat, fish, eels, and veal into wide-mouthed flasks—some open, some sealed with paper/wax, and in a second series covered with fine Naples veil (gauze). Flies laid eggs on top of the gauze; maggots hatched only where flies touched the meat.
3. **`:::scene Delft, 7 September 1674 & 17 September 1683`** — **TRUE.** Leeuwenhoek examined green water from Berkelse Mere (1674) and white plaque scraped from between his own front teeth (1683), writing to the Royal Society in London that *"there are more animals living in the scum on the teeth in a man's mouth than there are men in a whole kingdom"*. **HEDGE:** When he drank hot coffee, the bacteria in his front teeth died, while those in his back molars survived.
4. **`:::scene HMS Beagle, Galápagos Islands, September 1835`** — **LEGEND vs. TRUE.** **LEGEND:** That Darwin had an instant "Eureka!" moment looking at finch beaks on the Galápagos islands. **TRUE:** Darwin didn't even realise the birds were all finches while on the islands (he thought some were wrens, blackbirds, and grosbeaks) and forgot to label which island several specimens came from! Only in **March 1837 in London**, when ornithologist John Gould examined Darwin's skins and told him they were 13 closely related species of ground finches, did Darwin go back to Captain FitzRoy's properly labelled specimens and piece together how one ancestral flock had diverged across islands.
5. **`:::scene Khetwadi, Bombay, 1906`** — **TRUE.** Ten-year-old Salim Ali shot a sparrow with his toy airgun in Bombay, noticed an unusual yellow patch on its throat, and was taken by his uncle Amiruddin Tyabji to W. S. Millard at the Bombay Natural History Society (BNHS). Millard identified it as a Yellow-throated Sparrow (*Gymnoris xanthocollis*) and showed him drawers of stuffed Indian birds—launching Indian field ornithology.
6. **`:::scene Presidency College, Calcutta, 10 May 1901`** — **TRUE.** J. C. Bose demonstrated before the Royal Society in London (and in Calcutta) that plants (*Mimosa pudica* and *Desmodium gyrans*) respond to mechanical, chemical, and thermal stimuli with measurable electrical impulses that are blocked by anaesthetics (chloroform) and stopped permanently by poison (potassium bromide).
7. **`:::scene Colaba, Bombay (TIFR), 1962`** — **TRUE.** Homi Bhabha invited 30-year-old geneticist Obaid Siddiqi—at Leo Szilard's recommendation—to start the Molecular Biology Unit inside the physics-dominated Tata Institute of Fundamental Research on Mumbai's seafront.
