# School Wording & Bridge Table: Cambridge Lower Secondary (0893), IGCSE Biology (0610), IB MYP & NCERT

Checked 2026-10-30 against the **Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus (2026–2028)** and **Cambridge Lower Secondary Science 0893**.

In *The Living World*, we never water down first-principles mechanisms for an exam mark scheme, **but** we include a kind `:::school` bridge wherever Cambridge IGCSE / IB MYP uses specific mandatory terminology or simplified conventions.

| Topic & Chapter | Our First-Principles Explanation | Cambridge IGCSE 0610 / Lower Secondary Wording (`:::school` Bridge) | Examiner's Trap to Warn Her About (`:::careful`) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ch 1: Characteristics of Living Organisms** | Living systems are self-maintaining, cellular machines that use external free energy (coupled via ATP) to maintain low internal entropy and replicate digital instructions (DNA). | **MRS GREN** (Topic 1.1): *Movement, Respiration, Sensitivity, Growth, Reproduction, Excretion, Nutrition*. Every IGCSE paper expects exact definitions (e.g., *Excretion* = removal of waste products of metabolism and substances in excess; *Nutrition* = taking in materials for energy, growth and development). | **Trap 1:** Calling **egestion** (passing undigested fibre as faeces) *excretion*. Faeces never entered your body cells' chemical reactions! Excretion is $\text{CO}_2$ from lungs and urea/water/salts from kidneys/sweat.<br>**Trap 2:** Defining *respiration* as "breathing". In IGCSE, respiration is strictly *"the chemical reactions in cells that break down nutrient molecules and release energy for metabolism"*. |
| **Ch 1: Viruses & Classification** | Viruses are acellular genetic packages (RNA or DNA inside a protein capsid, sometimes with a lipid envelope) that have zero ribosomes or ATP synthesis of their own and execute only inside a host cell. | Topic 1.2 & 1.3: Five Kingdoms (*Prokaryote, Protoctist, Fungus, Plant, Animal*); Viruses are explicitly classified as **non-living** on the grounds that they do not carry out the seven MRS GREN processes on their own and consist only of a **protein coat** and **genetic material**. | **Trap:** Calling a virus a "cell" or a "bacterium", or saying it has a cell membrane or cytoplasm. |
| **Ch 2: Cell Structure & Magnification** | Cells are bounded 3D lipid-bilayer compartments packed with macromolecular machinery; eukaryotic organelles partition incompatible chemical reactions. | Topic 2.1 & 2.2: Animal vs. Plant vs. Bacterial cells (cell wall, cell membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus, vacuoles, mitochondria, chloroplasts, ribosomes, circular DNA + **plasmids** in bacteria). Formula: $\text{Magnification} = \text{image size} \div \text{actual size}$ ($M = I / A$, using $1\text{ mm} = 1000\text{ }\mu\text{m}$). | **Trap:** Saying plant cells have chloroplasts and lack mitochondria! Root hair cells and onion bulb cells have **zero chloroplasts** (they are underground in the dark), and **all** living plant cells have mitochondria to respire 24 hours a day. |
| **Ch 3: Diffusion, Osmosis & Active Transport** | Random thermal motion ($k_B T$) of particles causes net flux down a concentration or chemical potential gradient; membranes with aquaporins pass small water molecules while blocking hydrated ions/sucrose. | Topic 3.1–3.3: Extended IGCSE defines **osmosis** using **water potential**: *"the net movement of water molecules from a region of higher water potential (dilute solution) to a region of lower water potential (concentrated solution), through a partially permeable membrane"*. Terms: **turgid, turgor pressure, flaccid, plasmolysis**. | **Trap:** Saying *"water moves from a high concentration of solution to a low concentration"*. A concentrated sugar solution has a *low* concentration of free water molecules (low water potential)! Always say **water potential** or **dilute to concentrated solution**. |
| **Ch 4: Biological Molecules & Enzymes** | Enzymes are folded polypeptide chains whose 3D active-site pocket stabilises the transition state and lowers activation energy ($E_a$). Thermal agitation above $T_{\text{opt}}$ breaks weak intramolecular bonds, unfolding the pocket. | Topic 4.1 & 5.1: Food tests (**Benedict's** for reducing sugars, **iodine** for starch, **biuret** for protein, **ethanol emulsion** for fats, **DCPIP** for vitamin C). Enzymes: **substrate, active site, enzyme-substrate complex, complementary shape, optimum temperature/pH, denaturation**. | **Trap:** Never write *"high temperature kills the enzyme"*. Examiners deduct the mark immediately. Enzymes were never alive; write: *"kinetic energy increases, bonds holding the enzyme's 3D shape break, the active site changes shape and is no longer complementary to the substrate—the enzyme is **denatured**."* |
| **Ch 5: Photosynthesis** | Light-driven oxidation of $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ in thylakoid membranes generates ATP and NADPH, which the Calvin cycle uses to fix $\text{CO}_2$ into triose phosphate / glucose. | Topic 6.1: *"Photosynthesis is the process by which plants synthesise carbohydrates from raw materials using energy from light"*. Balanced equation: $6\text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\text{O}_2$ in the presence of light and chlorophyll (which *transfers light energy into chemical energy*). | **Trap:** Writing that plants *"make energy"* during photosynthesis. Energy cannot be created or destroyed (*The Moving World* Ch 7); chlorophyll **transfers light energy into chemical energy** stored in carbohydrates. |
| **Ch 6: Cellular Respiration** | Stepwise oxidation of carbon fuels coupled to proton pumping across the inner mitochondrial membrane to spin ATP synthase. | Topic 12.1–12.3: **Aerobic respiration** ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\text{O}_2 \to 6\text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O}$) vs. **Anaerobic respiration** in yeast ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 \to 2\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} + 2\text{CO}_2$) and in human muscle ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 \to 2\text{C}_3\text{H}_6\text{O}_3$, lactic acid), plus **oxygen debt** removed in the liver. | **Trap:** Writing *"respiration produces energy"*. IGCSE mark schemes insist on *"respiration **releases** energy"* (from chemical stores in glucose). |
| **Ch 11–13: DNA, Mitosis, Meiosis & Inheritance** | DNA is a double-stranded antiparallel polymer of four nucleotides whose sequence of triplets specifies the amino-acid sequence of polypeptides folded at ribosomes. | Topic 17 & 18: **Chromosome, gene, allele, dominant, recessive, homozygous, heterozygous, genotype, phenotype, mitosis** (genetically identical diploid cells for growth/repair), **meiosis** (reduction division producing genetically different haploid gametes). | **Trap:** Confusing **gene** (*a length of DNA that codes for a protein*) with **allele** (*an alternative version of a gene*). |
| **Ch 14: Variation & Selection** | Random mutations generate heritable variation; differential survival and reproduction shifts allele frequencies across generations. | Topic 18.3: IGCSE 6-step mark-scheme recipe for **natural selection**: (1) genetic variation within populations, (2) production of many offspring, (3) struggle for survival / competition for resources, (4) better-adapted individuals survive, (5) reproduce and (6) pass on their alleles to the next generation. | **Trap:** Writing *"bacteria mutate in order to become resistant to the antibiotic"*. Mutation is **random** and happens before/independently of the antibiotic; the antibiotic is the **selection pressure**. |
