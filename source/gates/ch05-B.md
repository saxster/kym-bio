### 5.5 Pulling water upwards without a heart

**Three-sentence explain-back:** Water evaporating inside a leaf changes tiny water surfaces and lowers liquid pressure, and attraction between connected water molecules transmits the pull through xylem so water replaces the loss from below. Xylem carries water and mineral ions in supported hollow passages, and a broken liquid connection can interrupt the route. In living phloem, loading sugar brings in water and raises source pressure, so sap flows towards a sink where material is removed, which can be above or below the source in separate pathways.

**Result: PASS.** The pull begins with a physical change at wet leaf surfaces rather than “roots suck”; cohesion is given an actual role in transmitting tension. Evaporation, vapour diffusion, osmosis and liquid bulk flow are distinguished. Source and sink roles precede the direction rule, active loading is a supported example rather than a universal claim, and the two phloem directions belong to different paths. Both central cards follow their mechanisms. The chapter's account avoids inherited source errors concerning gravitational water potential, universal night closure and one loading route; the reviewer does not endorse those errors in the underlying source. The relevant comparison is [OpenStax Biology 2e §30.5](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/30-5-transport-of-water-and-solutes-in-plants), read with the grounding note's explicit corrections.
