## 2. Mandatory ≤ 3-Sentence Explain-Backs (Sections `13.1`–`13.4`)

Each section explain-back below is **exactly 3 sentences** (verified ≤ 3 sentences) in 12-year-old Kym's voice and captures the complete causal mechanism from the prose alone without reproducing any target misconception:

- **Section 13.1 (`13.1 The monk in the pea patch: why traits don't blend, and how genes come in alleles` — 3 sentences):**
  Inheritance is the transmission of genetic information from generation to generation via genes—lengths of DNA at fixed chromosome loci that code for proteins—and alternative forms of a gene called alleles (`R` vs `r`), which sit in pairs on maternal and paternal homologous chromosomes in diploid (`2n`) cells and separate into single copies in haploid (`n`) gametes during meiosis. Johann Gregor Mendel disproved paint-pot blending inheritance between 1856 and 1866 by crossing pure-breeding pea lines and scoring `19,959` `F₂` outcomes (`14,949` dominant : `5,010` recessive = `2.98 : 1`) plus `F₃` self-crosses (`193` true-breeding `RR` : `372` segregating `Rr`), proving that a recessive allele stays completely intact inside a heterozygous `F₁` hybrid (`Rr`) and reappears unblemished in `25%` (`rr`) of `F₂` offspring. At the molecular level, a homozygous dominant (`RR`, `100%` starch-branching enzyme `SBEI`) pea seed and a heterozygous (`Rr`, `50%` haplosufficient `SBEI` dose) pea seed both convert sugars into branched amylopectin starch and share the round phenotype, whereas a homozygous recessive (`rr`, `0%` active `SBEI` due to a `0.8 kb` insertion) seed accumulates sucrose, swells by osmosis, and wrinkles as it dries.

- **Section 13.2 (`13.2 The Punnett ledger: monohybrid crosses, test crosses, and family pedigrees` — 3 sentences):**
  Reginald Punnett's `2 × 2` genetic diagram tracks how meiosis separates a diploid parent's two alleles into single circled haploid gametes (`(B)` and `(b)`, never two alleles in one gamete) and how random fertilisation recombines them independently on every pregnancy (`Bb × Bb → 1 BB : 2 Bb : 1 bb` genotypes = `3` dominant : `1` recessive phenotypes, with a `2/3` carrier probability among already-born unaffected offspring). Because homozygous dominant (`BB`) and heterozygous (`Bb`) individuals look identical, breeders unmask an unknown dominant genotype using Mendel's diagnostic test cross against a homozygous recessive partner (`bb`), where `100%` dominant offspring (`Bb`) proves the parent is `BB` and a `1 : 1` split (`50% Bb : 50% bb`, or even a single `bb` offspring) proves the parent is `Bb`. In human family pedigrees—such as those mapped in Mumbai by L. D. Sanghvi—two unaffected parents having an affected child (`Aa × Aa → aa`) proves the condition is autosomal recessive, whereas two affected parents having an unaffected child (`Aa × Aa → aa`) proves the condition is autosomal dominant.

- **Section 13.3 (`13.3 When neither allele yields: codominance and the ABO blood group puzzle` — 3 sentences):**
  Codominance occurs when both alleles in a heterozygous organism are expressed independently and simultaneously in the phenotype without blending, so crossing two codominant heterozygotes (`IᴬIᴮ × IᴬIᴮ` in human ABO blood groups or `CᴿCᵂ × CᴿCᵂ` in roan cattle) produces a `1 : 2 : 1` phenotype ratio that directly mirrors the `1 : 2 : 1` genotype ratio. Although three alleles (`Iᴬ`, `Iᴮ`, and `Iᴼ`) of the `ABO` gene on Chromosome `9` circulate in the human population, each diploid person inherits only two alleles (`6` possible genotypes across `4` blood groups): `Iᴬ` codes for `GTA` transferase (adding GalNAc = Antigen A), `Iᴮ` codes for `GTB` transferase (adding D-Gal = Antigen B), and `Iᴼ` carries a `ΔG261` frameshift loss-of-function deletion leaving H-antigen stalks unmodified. Because `Iᴬ` and `Iᴮ` are codominant with each other (`IᴬIᴮ` = Group AB) and both are completely dominant over recessive `Iᴼ` (`IᴬIᴼ` = Group A; `IᴮIᴼ` = Group B; `IᴼIᴼ` = Group O), a heterozygous `IᴬIᴼ × IᴮIᴼ` couple can have children of all four blood groups (`25%` each), whereas a Group AB parent (`IᴬIᴮ`) has no `Iᴼ` allele and can never have a Group O (`IᴼIᴼ`) child.

- **Section 13.4 (`13.4 The twenty-third pair: XX, XY, and sex-linked inheritance` — 3 sentences):**
  While `22` pairs of human chromosomes are matched autosomes, the `23rd` pair consists of sex chromosomes where females are `XX` (making `100%` `X` ova) and males are `XY` (making `50%` `X` sperm and `50%` `Y` sperm), locking every fertilisation to a `1 : 1` (`50% XX : 50% XY`) sex ratio in which the father gives his `X` chromosome to `100%` of his daughters and his `Y` chromosome to `100%` of his sons (`0%` father-to-son `X` transmission). A sex-linked characteristic is controlled by a gene on a sex chromosome—typically on the non-homologous region of the large `X` chromosome (`~156 Mb`, `~850–900` genes including retinal cone opsin `Xᴮ/Xᵇ` and blood-clotting Factor VIII/IX `Xᴴ/Xʰ`) that has no corresponding locus on the short `Y` chromosome (`~57 Mb`, `<100` genes). Because an `XY` male has only one `X` chromosome (hemizygous), he cannot be an unaffected carrier and expresses X-linked recessive conditions like red–green colour vision deficiency (`XᵇY`) or haemophilia (`XʰY`) from a single recessive allele inherited from his carrier mother (`XᴮXᵇ × XᴮY → 50%` of sons affected = `25%` of all children), whereas a female (`XX`) is affected (`XᵇXᵇ`) only if she inherits a recessive allele from both her mother and an affected father.
