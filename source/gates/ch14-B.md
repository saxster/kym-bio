## 2. Mandatory ≤ 3-Sentence Explain-Backs (Sections `14.1`–`14.4`)

Each section explain-back below is **exactly 3 sentences** (verified ≤ 3 sentences) in 12-year-old Kym's voice and captures the complete causal mechanism from the prose alone without reproducing any target misconception:

- **Section 14.1 (`14.1 The glitch engine: continuous vs discontinuous variation, mutations and mutagens` — 3 sentences):**
  Variation—any phenotypic or genetic difference between individuals of the same species—splits into continuous variation (such as student height or seed mass, where many polygenic loci plus environmental factors like nutrition produce an unbroken range of intermediates plotted as touching histogram bars under a bell curve) and discontinuous variation (such as `ABO` blood groups or pea seed coats, where `1–2` genes with `0%` environmental shift snap individuals into distinct categories plotted as separated bars). While meiosis (crossing over and `2²³` independent assortment), random mating, and random fertilisation (`~70.4 trillion` diploid combinations) reshuffle existing alleles into new genotypes, only mutation—a random change in DNA base sequence (`CCT GAG GAG → CCT GTG GAG`, `Glu⁶ → Val⁶` in `HBB` creating `Hbˢ`) or chromosome number—invents brand-new alleles, and somatic environmental changes are never inherited because they do not alter gamete DNA. Ionising radiation (`50×`), UV-B light (`8×`), and chemical mutagens (`20×`) raise the frequency of random DNA glitches without ever directing which gene mutates, as Joshua and Esther Lederberg proved in `1952` by velveteen replica plating when colonies `C3`, `C7`, and `C11` on an antibiotic-free master plate were shown to be `100%` resistant before ever touching streptomycin or penicillin.

- **Section 14.2 (`14.2 The six-step filter: natural selection, peppered moths and antibiotic resistance` — 3 sentences):**
  Natural selection, discovered independently by Charles Robert Darwin and Alfred Russel Wallace (`1858`), is an automatic six-step filter in which (1) pre-existing genetic variation within a population meets (2) overproduction of offspring and (3) a struggle for existence under environmental selection pressures, driving (4) differential survival of better-adapted individuals, (5) reproduction and inheritance of advantageous alleles by survivors, and (6) an increase in advantageous allele frequency over generations (evolution). In Bernard Kettlewell's (`1953–1955`) and Michael Majerus's (`2001–2007`, `4,864` moths) *Biston betularia* studies, insectivorous birds selectively ate pale `typica` moths on soot-darkened Birmingham bark (`90–98%` dark `carbonaria` by `1895`) and then reversed allele frequencies back to `88–95%` pale `typica` after post-`1956` Clean Air laws restored pale lichens, without a single moth choosing to change colour. In bacterial infections such as MRSA, antibiotics never cause mutations and bacteria never become "immune"; instead, stopping a `7-day` antibiotic course early on Day `3` kills the `99%` sensitive competitors and leaves the `1%` pre-existing resistant mutants alive to divide every `20` minutes into a `99.4%` drug-resistant relapse, whereas completing the full course alongside host phagocytes clears `100%` of the bacterial load and refusing antibiotics for acellular viruses prevents needless selection pressure.

- **Section 14.3 (`14.3 Built for the extremes: adaptive features of xerophytes, hydrophytes and animals` — 3 sentences):**
  An adaptive feature is an inherited feature that helps an organism survive and reproduce in its environment, raising its fitness—the probability of surviving and reproducing in the environment in which it is found (unlike short-term, non-heritable physiological acclimatisation). Xerophytes such as marram grass (*Ammophila arenaria*) and cacti (*Opuntia*) conserve `92%` of water in dry dunes and deserts via thick waxy cuticles (`~8.5 µm` vs `2.2 µm` in bean), C-rolled blades with sunken stomatal pits and interlocking trichome hairs that trap moist air to flatten the water potential gradient (`ΔΨ`), spine leaves with green succulent stems, and deep (`3.2 m`) or widespread roots, whereas floating hydrophytes such as water lilies (*Nymphaea*) thrive in water via large buoyant `aerenchyma` air spaces (`~58%` volume) that channel `O₂` down to roots in anaerobic mud, stomata on the upper epidermis only (`~280 mm⁻²` upper vs `0 mm⁻²` lower), thin/absent cuticles (`~0.4 µm`), and reduced roots (`0.25 m`) and xylem. Similarly, the Thar Desert camel combines a long kidney loop of Henle, a fat-concentrating dorsal hump that lets the rest of its skin radiate heat, wide low-pressure foot pads, and a `34–41 °C` thermal tolerance window, whereas the Arctic polar bear minimises heat loss in `−40 °C` pack ice with a low surface-area-to-volume (`SA : V`) ratio, `10 cm` subcutaneous blubber, hollow camouflaging guard hairs, and limb countercurrent heat exchange.

- **Section 14.4 (`14.4 Guided evolution and deep time: selective breeding, fossils and DNA clocks` — 3 sentences):**
  Selective breeding (artificial selection)—exemplified by E. K. Janaki Ammal crossing sweet *Saccharum officinarum* with hardy wild *S. spontaneum* in Coimbatore (`8.2% → 16.4%` sucrose), Gir dairy cattle breeding (`1,350 L → 3,650 L`), and six *Brassica oleracea* vegetables—follows a five-step human loop: (1) choose a desirable feature, (2) select parents showing it strongly, (3) cross-breed them, (4) select the best offspring, and (5) repeat over many generations. Unlike natural selection, where the environment selects traits that raise wild fitness while maintaining broader diversity, selective breeding uses human choice to rapidly amplify human-favoured traits while narrowing the gene pool (`heterozygosity drops`), which unmasks harmful recessive alleles (`Aa × Aa → 25% aa`, inbreeding depression) and leaves uniform crops vulnerable to new pathogens unless crossed back with wild relatives. Across deep geological time, Birbal Sahni's Jurassic *Pentoxyleae* and Permian *Glossopteris* Gondwana fossils match the molecular clock of DNA base sequences, Carl Woese's (`1977`) `16S` rRNA Three-Domain tree (*Bacteria*, *Archaea*, *Eukarya*), and universal proteins (`0/104` Cytochrome c and `0/146` beta-globin amino acid differences between humans and chimpanzees, `98.8%` coding DNA identity, `~6.5 Ma` split, vs `11` in dogs and `24` in dogfish sharks), proving that species with more similar DNA base sequences share a more recent common ancestor.
