## 2. Mandatory ≤ 3-Sentence Explain-Backs (Sections `15.1`–`15.4`)

Each section explain-back below is **exactly 3 sentences** (verified ≤ 3 sentences) in 12-year-old Kym's voice and captures the complete causal mechanism from the prose alone without reproducing any target misconception:

- **Section 15.1 (`15.1 Sunlit chains & the ten-percent tax: food webs, ecological pyramids and population growth` — 3 sentences):**
  In an ecosystem—a community of interacting organisms plus their physical environment—the Sun is the principal energy input, and food-chain arrows (`Producer → Primary consumer → Secondary consumer → Tertiary consumer`) always point in the direction of one-way energy flow before respiration dissipates that energy as unusable thermal heat, while decomposers recycle chemical matter. Because roughly `90%` of energy is lost at every trophic step via aerobic respiration and heat (`~54%`), indigestible egested faeces (`~22%`), excreted urea in urine, and uneaten parts, food chains rarely exceed `4–5` levels, eating crop plants directly delivers `~10×` more energy per hectare than feeding crops to livestock, and a pyramid of energy (`kJ m⁻² yr⁻¹`) is always upright even when a single giant banyan tree (`1 < 8,000` insects) inverts the pyramid of numbers over an upright pyramid of dry biomass. When a population grows in a closed vessel, environmental limiting factors (food supply, competition, predation, disease, and toxic waste) shape a four-phase sigmoid (`S`-shaped) curve: a slow lag phase, a steep exponential (`log`) phase (`birth rate ≫ death rate`), a flat stationary phase at carrying capacity `K` (`birth rate = death rate`), and a death phase (`death rate > birth rate`).

- **Section 15.2 (`15.2 Atoms on loan: the water, carbon and nitrogen cycles` — 3 sentences):**
  Unlike one-way energy, water and carbon atoms cycle continuously: solar evaporation, leaf stomatal transpiration, cloud condensation, and monsoon precipitation circulate `H₂O`, while photosynthesis is the single biological process removing atmospheric `CO₂` (`~0.04%`, rising from `315 ppm` in `1958` to `426 ppm` on Keeling's curve) and aerobic respiration, decomposer decay, and fossil-fuel combustion return `CO₂` to the air. Although `78%` of air is `N₂` gas, plants cannot split its `N≡N` triple covalent bond through their leaves and instead absorb soil nitrate ions (`NO₃⁻`) through root hair cells by active transport to synthesize amino acids and proteins. Four microbial guilds run the nitrogen cycle: nitrogen-fixing bacteria (*Rhizobium* shielded by pink leghaemoglobin inside legume root nodules and free-living soil *Azotobacter*, alongside lightning) convert `N₂ → NH₄⁺`, decomposers break down dead proteins, faeces, and liver-deaminated urea into `NH₄⁺` (ammonification), aerobic nitrifying bacteria in drained, ploughed soil oxidise `NH₄⁺ → NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻` (nitrification), and anaerobic denitrifying bacteria in waterlogged soil reduce `NO₃⁻` back into escaping `N₂` gas.

- **Section 15.3 (`15.3 Footprints on the web: farming, deforestation, eutrophication and conservation` — 3 sentences):**
  While agricultural machinery, `N-P-K` chemical fertilisers, insecticides, herbicides, and selective breeding boost food production, large-scale monocultures slash biodiversity and invite pest explosions, intensive livestock pens risk disease spread and antibiotic resistance, and deforestation triggers four linked harms: species extinction, severe topsoil erosion, river siltation and flash flooding from lost transpiration, and rising atmospheric `CO₂`. When leached nitrate fertilisers or untreated sewage enter a lake such as Powai Lake, they trigger a seven-step eutrophication cascade: (1) excess `NO₃⁻`/mineral ions fuel (2) a surface algal bloom that (3) blocks sunlight to submerged plants so (4) submerged plants die, (5) aerobic decomposer bacteria multiply on the dead detritus, (6) bacterial aerobic respiration strips dissolved `O₂` (high `BOD`), and (7) fish suffocate—alongside marine harm from non-biodegradable plastics, food-chain bioaccumulation, and the enhanced greenhouse effect driven by `CO₂` (fossil fuels, deforestation) and `CH₄` (cattle, rice paddies, landfills). Maintaining sustainable resources (produced as rapidly as removed) requires selective logging and reforestation in forests, catch quotas, minimum net mesh sizes (letting juveniles escape to breed), and closed seasons in fisheries, and protecting endangered species from inbreeding depression (`aa` homozygotes in genetic bottlenecks) via habitat protection, education, `-20 °C` seed banks, and zoo captive breeding with artificial insemination (`AI`) and `IVF`.

- **Section 15.4 (`15.4 Living factories & rewritable code: fermenters, plasmids, recombinant insulin and CRISPR` — 3 sentences):**
  Bacteria and fungi excel in biotechnology because they divide rapidly (every `20 min`), synthesize complex molecules using the universal triplet genetic code, raise few ethical concerns, and carry circular plasmids, powering everyday processes from yeast anaerobic respiration (`C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂`, where `CO₂` raises bread dough while oven heat evaporates the ethanol, or distilling bioethanol fuel) to pectinase clarifying fruit juice, `30–40 °C` protease/lipase biological washing powders, and alginate-immobilised lactase making lactose-free milk (`lactose → glucose + galactose`). Industrial stainless-steel fermenters grow *Penicillium* (penicillin), *Fusarium* (mycoprotein), and GM *E. coli* at scale by controlling six variables: high-pressure steam sterilization (aseptic precautions against competitors), glucose and ammonium/nitrate nutrient inlets, a cold-water cooling jacket to remove microbial respiratory heat and prevent enzyme denaturation, pH buffers, a sterile oxygen sparger for aerobic ATP, and motorised stirring paddles. To manufacture recombinant human insulin (`0610 21.3`), scientists (1) cut the human insulin gene with a restriction enzyme leaving single-stranded sticky ends, (2) cut a bacterial plasmid vector with the **exact same restriction enzyme** to produce complementary sticky ends, (3) join them with DNA ligase into a recombinant plasmid, (4) transform host *E. coli* bacteria, and (5) clone them in a fermenter—a single-gene cross-kingdom transfer that is faster and more precise than multi-generation selective breeding and is now complemented by GM crops (`Bt` cotton, Golden Rice) and programmable `CRISPR-Cas9` guide-RNA genome editing.
