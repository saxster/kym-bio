## 2. Mandatory ≤ 3-Sentence Explain-Backs (Sections `10.1`–`10.4`)

Each section explain-back below is **exactly 3 sentences** (verified ≤ 3 sentences) in 12-year-old Kym's voice and captures the complete causal mechanism from the prose alone without any target misconception:

- **Section 10.1 (`10.1 The thermostat and the pantry: homeostasis, negative feedback, and blood glucose` — 3 sentences):**
  To keep my brain and body cells safe from osmotic shrinking, swelling, or running out of fuel, homeostasis holds my internal environment near narrow set points using negative feedback, where any drift triggers an effector response in the opposite direction. When my blood glucose rises above ~5.0 mmol/L after eating sweet Alphonso *aamras*, beta cells in my pancreas islets secrete the protein hormone insulin so liver and muscle cells absorb glucose and link it into insoluble glycogen (`glucose → glycogen`), whereas when blood glucose drops during a cricket match, alpha cells secrete glucagon so my liver breaks glycogen back down into soluble glucose (`glycogen → glucose`). In Type 1 diabetes, the immune system destroys the beta cells so blood glucose climbs dangerously high and spills into urine, which is why patients test their blood glucose, regulate meals and exercise, and inject insulin under the skin so stomach pepsin and intestinal trypsin do not digest the protein hormone first.

- **Section 10.2 (`10.2 38 °C in Dadar: skin structure, evaporative cooling, and thermoregulation` — 3 sentences):**
  My brain's hypothalamus monitors blood temperature directly and receives warning impulses from skin receptors to hold my core temperature near 37.0 °C so my enzymes neither slow down in the cold nor denature above 40–42 °C. When I walk in 38 °C Dadar heat, the smooth muscle in the walls of my skin arterioles relaxes (vasodilation) to let more warm blood flow through my fixed surface capillary loops while coiled sweat glands secrete sweat whose water evaporates from my epidermis, carrying away 2,420 J of latent heat per gram (though in 80% Mumbai humidity evaporation slows down and dripping sweat removes zero heat). When I get cold, my skin arterioles vasoconstrict to keep blood deep beneath my insulating subcutaneous fatty tissue, sweating stops, hair erector muscles contract to stand hairs upright, and shivering skeletal muscles respire glucose faster to release thermal energy.

- **Section 10.3 (`10.3 The chemical cleanup crew: liver deamination, urea, and the kidney’s two-stage nephron ledger` — 3 sentences):**
  Because my body cannot store spare amino acids from *dal tadka*, my liver assimilates the amino acids it needs into proteins and deaminates the excess by removing their nitrogen-containing amino part (`−NH₂`) to form toxic ammonia and convert it into urea (`(NH₂)₂CO`), which travels in blood plasma to my kidneys for excretion alongside lung excretion of `CO₂` (unlike egestion of undigested faeces that never entered cells). Inside each kidney's cortex and medulla, a million nephrons clean blood in two stages: high blood pressure in the glomerulus forces water, glucose, urea, and mineral ions into the cup-shaped renal capsule (`180 L/day`) while holding back red blood cells and large plasma proteins. As that filtrate flows along the renal tubule and collecting duct, active-transport carriers and aquaporins selectively reabsorb 100% of the glucose (up to the ~10.5 mmol/L carrier saturation limit), needed mineral ions, and over 99% of the water back into surrounding capillaries, leaving urea concentrated ~67-fold with excess water and excess ions as `1.5 L/day` of urine flowing down the ureters to the bladder and out through the urethra.

- **Section 10.4 (`10.4 The invisible border guard: pathogens, barriers, cholera, antibodies, and immunological memory` — 3 sentences):**
  Transmissible pathogens spread by direct contact or indirectly via contaminated water, food, surfaces, air, or vectors, and before reaching tissues they must get past community hygiene defences plus my body's mechanical barriers (keratinised skin, nose hairs) and chemical barriers (airway mucus, stomach `HCl` acid)—and if *Vibrio cholerae* reaches the small intestine and secretes cholera toxin that drives `Cl⁻` and osmotic water loss into the gut, oral rehydration solution (water with measured salt and glucose) rescues water via intact `Na⁺`–glucose cotransporters. When a pathogen enters blood or tissue fluid, lobed-nucleus phagocytes engulf and digest microbes non-specifically by phagocytosis, while round-nucleus lymphocytes recognise the specific 3D shape of the pathogen's surface antigens and secrete protein antibodies with complementary binding sites that destroy pathogens directly or mark them for phagocytes. Catching an infection or receiving a vaccine (weakened/dead pathogens or their antigens) stimulates my own lymphocytes to make antibodies and long-lived memory cells for long-term active immunity, whereas receiving ready-made antibodies across the placenta, in breast milk, or by antivenom injection gives immediate short-term passive immunity because no memory cells are produced.
