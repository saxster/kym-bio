## 2. Mandatory ≤ 3-Sentence Explain-Backs (Sections `12.1`–`12.4`)

Each section explain-back below is **exactly 3 sentences** (verified ≤ 3 sentences) in 12-year-old Kym's voice and captures the complete causal mechanism from the prose alone without reproducing any target misconception:

- **Section 12.1 (`12.1 Unzipping the ladder: semi-conservative DNA replication` — 3 sentences):**
  Before a cell divides, it copies every chromosome during the S phase of interphase by semi-conservative replication: DNA helicase unzips the hydrogen bonds between complementary bases (`A=T` and `C≡G`), and DNA polymerase builds a new partner strand against each original template strand across `~40,000` replication origins with `~10⁻⁹` proofreading accuracy. Matthew Meselson and Franklin Stahl proved this in 1958 by shifting `¹⁵N`-heavy *E. coli* into light `¹⁴N` broth and spinning the DNA in `CsCl` at `140,000 × g`, showing `100%` hybrid `¹⁵N/¹⁴N` DNA (`1.717 g/cm³`) at Generation 1 (ruling out conservative copying) and `50%` hybrid + `50%` light (`1.710 g/cm³`) at Generation 2 (ruling out dispersive copying). Immediately after S phase, a human diploid cell still has `46` chromosomes (`2n = 46`) because biologists count chromosomes by their `46` shared centromeres, even though each chromosome now consists of two identical sister chromatids (`92` DNA double helices in total, each half old and half new).

- **Section 12.2 (`12.2 The thread dance: mitosis, growth, repair and asexual cloning` — 3 sentences):**
  Mitosis is nuclear division giving rise to genetically identical cells: after the `46` chromosomes replicate into `92` sister chromatids before mitosis begins, they condense into threads, line up individually along the cell equator, and have their copies pulled apart by spindle fibres to opposite poles so both daughter nuclei keep the exact diploid chromosome number (`2n = 46 → 2n = 46`). Multicellular organisms rely on mitosis for four jobs—growth (such as a zygote growing into `37 trillion` cells or an onion root-tip apical meristem lengthening with an `8–12%` mitotic index), repair of damaged tissues, replacement of worn-out cells (such as nucleated bone-marrow stem cells making `2 million` red blood cells per second because mature enucleated red blood cells cannot divide), and asexual reproduction from a single parent. Asexual clones like sprouted potato tuber eyes, strawberry runners, and Cavendish banana suckers preserve `100%` of a parent crop's desirable traits and allow rapid one-parent colonisation in the wild, but their zero genetic variation leaves whole monoculture fields vulnerable if a single pathogen like potato blight or banana `TR4` strikes.

- **Section 12.3 (`12.3 Same library, different bookmarks: stem cells and selective gene expression` — 3 sentences):**
  Stem cells—including pluripotent embryonic stem cells, multipotent adult tissue stem cells in bone marrow and gut crypts, and lifelong plant apical meristems—are unspecialised cells that divide by mitosis to self-renew and produce daughter cells that can become specialised for specific functions. Because every nucleated body cell descends from the zygote by mitosis, a pancreatic beta cell, an immature bone-marrow erythroblast, and a spinal motor neurone all keep the exact same `46` chromosomes and `~20,000` genes without ever deleting unused DNA, as proved when John Gurdon (1962) grew fertile frogs from tadpole intestinal nuclei and Shinya Yamanaka (2006) rewound adult skin fibroblasts into pluripotent stem cells (`iPSCs`) using four transcription factors (`Oct4, Sox2, Klf4, c-Myc`). Those cells build different proteins through selective gene expression: all living cells express housekeeping genes like `ATP5F1A`, while tissue-specific transcription factors switch ON only the `INS` gene in beta cells to make insulin mRNA and protein, only `HBB` in erythroblasts to make haemoglobin, and only `CHAT` and sodium-channel genes in motor neurones while keeping the unneeded genes coiled and silent.

- **Section 12.4 (`12.4 Halving the deck: meiosis, gametes and fertilisation` — 3 sentences):**
  If reproductive organs made sperm and egg gametes by mitosis (`2n = 46`), then fertilisation—the fusion of gamete nuclei—would double the human chromosome count every generation (`46 → 92 → 184 → 47,104` by Generation 10), so testes, ovaries, anthers, and ovules use meiosis, a reduction division that halves the chromosome number from diploid (`2n = 46`, two sets of `23` homologous pairs) to haploid (`n = 23`, a single set containing one chromosome from each pair). After one S-phase DNA replication (`46` chromosomes = `92` sister chromatids), Meiosis I pairs maternal and paternal homologous chromosomes side by side for crossing over and separates them randomly (`2²³ ≈ 8.4 million` combinations), and Meiosis II separates the sister chromatids to yield four genetically different haploid gametes whose fusion at fertilisation (`n + n = 23 + 23 = 2n = 46`) restores the diploid zygote. Because Meiosis I requires homologous chromosomes to pair evenly two-by-two, an odd-numbered mule (`63` chromosomes) or a triploid Cavendish banana (`3n = 33`, `11` homologous triplets that split `2-vs-1` at random) jams during meiosis and produces sterile seedless fruit, whereas E. K. Janaki Ammal combined meiotic crosses with mitotic stem-cutting clones to breed hardy polyploid Coimbatore sugarcanes.
