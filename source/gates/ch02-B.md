### 2.3 The geometry trap

No explanatory fix required. A larger cube's greater total surface is explicitly reconciled with its smaller ratio, while distance, active volume and alternative shapes prevent an absolute cell-size rule. “Why should a cell care?” is a conversational question, not an explanation that assigns intention; the following sentences give the physical mechanism.

**Three-sentence explain-back:** When a cube's side doubles, its surface becomes four times as big but its inside becomes eight times as big. If each bit of inside needs a similar supply, there is less boundary serving it and a longer trip to the centre. Thin shapes, folds and internal transport can change the problem, so geometry does not say every large cell must die.
