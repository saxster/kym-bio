## 3.1 Blind bounces, not wishes

Sequence: crowded Mumbai train as invitation, explicitly limited analogy (people choose destinations; molecules do not); particles continually collide; equal probability of crossing from either side; more particles per equal volume means more expected crossings; net difference; name diffusion; at equal concentration continued crossings with no sustained net transfer. Oxygen enters respiring cells and CO₂ leaves when their respective concentration gradients permit. Diffusion does not require a membrane or ATP supply. Kinetic energy comes from thermal motion. A steeper gradient, larger exchange area, higher temperature (other conditions comparable) and shorter path increase rate. Distance matters strongly: random wandering cancels many steps, rather than taking a straight path.

Model numbers, not measured gas rates: equal-volume chambers, 100 tokens, 80 left/20 right. For a chosen 0.1 chance of changing side per interval: expected 8 left→right and 2 right→left, net 6; 50/50 gives 5 each way on average. Individual trials fluctuate; no exact 50/50 lock. Random walk widget conserves exactly 100 particles, reflects at walls and at closed divider, uses identical independent directions on both sides, counts actual crossings. Count whole batches, not frame-rate dependent displacement. No ballistic hard-sphere collision accuracy claimed. Re-seed/reset on initial-distribution control; no labelled seconds or real diffusivity.

Measured physical scale: exact Boltzmann constant k_B = 1.380649 × 10⁻²³ J/K, SI via NIST https://www.nist.gov/pml/special-publication-330/sp-330-section-2 . At chosen 300 K (about 27 °C), k_BT = 4.141947 × 10⁻²¹ J ≈ 4.14 × 10⁻²¹ J; characteristic thermal energy scale, NOT each molecule's fixed energy or full mean kinetic energy. Already computed with Python. No numeric motion speed derived from this.

Evidence: repeatable dye-spreading observation in still room-temperature water; currents can also move dye, so not a pure diffusion measurement. Simulation isolates a rule and tests its consequences; it is not empirical proof. Home: food colouring in cool water, no ammonia, perfume inhalation, hot water or flames.

0610 phrases: net movement; higher to lower concentration; down a concentration gradient; random movement; kinetic energy. Define concentration as amount per volume, not raw count when volumes differ.

## 3.2 Water potential and the selective gate

Sequence: membrane permeability is chemical selectivity as well as size. Lipid middle is unfavourable for ions surrounded by water; O₂/CO₂ can cross; water crosses lipid slowly and faster through water-selective proteins. Typical water-specific aquaporins block ions and sucrose; specific ion channels/carriers elsewhere can pass selected solutes. No claim all aquaporin family members carry only water. Aquaporin = water pore (Nobel).

Dissolving solute changes water's interactions and lowers its chemical potential; explain as less capacity for net outward water transfer at equal pressure. Define water potential as water's tendency to move, incorporating dissolved substances AND pressure, not a force or literal suction. Water keeps crossing both ways. Net water transfer through partially permeable membrane goes higher→lower water potential: osmosis. In dilute/concentrated comparisons assume equal pressure and non-crossing solute. Raised column pressure can halt net entry before equal solute concentration. Aquaporins speed passage, not create direction or use ATP as water pumps.

TRUE historical test: Preston, Carroll, Guggino & Agre, 1992, Appearance of water channels in Xenopus oocytes expressing red cell CHIP28 protein, Science 256:385–387, DOI https://doi.org/10.1126/science.256.5055.385 . Nobel scientist's first-person account https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2003/agre/biographical/ dates first test 9 October 1991 in Baltimore; public summary https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2003/8752-the-nobel-prize-in-chemistry-2003-2003-5/ describes 1992 publication. Engineered eggs in distilled water swell far faster than controls. Say controls changed little during comparison, not absolutely impermeable. No home animal experiment. Peter Agre born 1949, own Nobel biography. Cast story credits Greg Preston and William Guggino, no invented dialogue or emotional scene.

Ideal pressure example (chosen, not sap data): external water potential −0.30 MPa; cell solute contribution −0.80 MPa plus +0.50 MPa pressure gives −0.30 MPa. Zero NET flow, different solute concentrations. MPa is a pressure unit, 1 MPa = 1,000,000 Pa. For this short-distance model Ψ = solute + pressure; gravity/matrix omitted explicitly.

## 3.3 Balloons inside boxes

Water influx increases animal-cell volume; human RBC lacks cell wall and may lyse in sufficiently dilute water. Concentrated nonpenetrating solute outside causes water loss/shrivelling. Plant wall allows pressure to rise as vacuole takes up water; turgor supports soft tissues. Turgid = swollen/firm, flaccid = lacking turgor; plasmolysis specifically membrane-bound contents pulling away from wall after sufficient water loss, not wall shrinking or any slight wilting. An isotonic animal cell has no sustained net water change; turgid plant pressure complicates equal-solute shorthand. Microscopy before and after saline exposure reveals contracted contents inside largely fixed wall. Labels: wall, cell membrane, vacuole; different specimens at different enlargements. Folded 3D inspection compares outer wall and inner membrane, no molecular geometry or calibrated volume prediction.

Kitchen protocol choices: adult cuts at least six comparable raw-potato strips about 60 × 5 × 5 mm, three per cup, one potato. Equal initial lengths where possible, same time/temperature/liquid volumes, identical blotting and no squeezing. Tap water versus 5 g table salt made up to 100 mL final solution (5% mass/volume), not seawater. Weigh salt if scale available; if not, use a level teaspoon and label concentration approximate (spoon masses vary). Immerse, first observation 30 minutes, then up to 60 if unclear; record true results and replicate spread. Length may be too coarse for ruler; mass with suitable scale or relative bending supports observation but does not reveal plasmolysis directly. Rehydrate salty strips in fresh water, possible recovery not guaranteed after damage. No eating used potato, dispose same day, clean up, no blood, sharp tools adult only.

Formula percentage length change = 100 × (final−initial)/initial. Illustrative invented 60→63 mm = +5%; 60→56 = −6.67%, NOT predicted outcomes; omit these endpoints from main experiment. A 1 mm reading increment is about 1.67% of a 60 mm length; no false precision. Osmosis evidence supports interpretation, tissue variability/cut damage may interfere.

## 3.4 Active transport and the mangrove resolution

A downhill pathway alone cannot maintain accumulation against an unfavourable electrochemical gradient. ATP hydrolysis coupled to carrier shape changes can pump ions. Primary active transport uses ATP directly. In root hair cells a proton pump exports H⁺; stored proton electrochemical gradient can drive H⁺/nitrate cotransport, accumulating nitrate. Root nitrate is a secondary active transport example, not a fictional nitrate pump consuming one ATP per nitrate. Electrical attraction/repulsion matters for ions as well as concentration. No quantitative ATP cost assigned. Respiration supplies ATP; waterlogged roots can lose uptake capacity if oxygen shortage reduces aerobic ATP supply, not an absolute instant stop.

TRUE historical experiment: Jens C. Skou (1918–2018), Aarhus, 1957 publication, crab nerve membrane ATPase activity affected by Na⁺/K⁺; supported identification of ion-transporting machinery, not proof of every step from one broken-cell assay. First-person Nobel lecture (publisher abstract accessible) https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/(SICI)1521-3773(19980918)37:17%3C2320::AID-ANIE2320%3E3.0.CO;2-2 ; primary 1957 article reprint record https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2550074/ (1989 reprint must not be labelled original publication year). Nobel facts https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/1997/skou/facts/ . Use historical fact only, no copied graph. Cast story distinguishes animal sodium/potassium pump from root proton pump.

Mangroves: Maharashtra Forest Department lists Avicennia marina (grey mangrove, Tivar) across coastal districts: https://mangroves.maharashtra.gov.in/Site/1086/Mangrove-Diversity . Carter Road scene is a present-day reader invitation, no local salinity measurement or species identification from distance. Expert research synthesis Reef & Lovelock, Regulation of water balance in mangroves (2015), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4332601/ , DOI https://doi.org/10.1093/aob/mcu174 (indexed research text accessed; direct PMC shows CAPTCHA). Roots exclude much salt, not all. Accumulated ions compartmentalised in vacuoles plus compatible organic solutes support low water potential; leaf evaporation contributes xylem tension; water uptake is passive down total water potential. A. marina also secretes salt through leaf glands; not all mangroves do. No claim roots pump water uphill using ATP or filter by a coarse sieve. Do not quote a universal exclusion percentage. Reported A. marina daytime hydrostatic potentials down to −5 MPa in Waisel et al. 1986 are study-specific; not used as Carter Road measurement.

Case: NOAA https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/drinksw.html explains salt load requires water for excretion; seawater drinking can cause net water loss/dehydration. No salt drinking trial, no invented urine concentration/arithmetic. Osmosis explains cell-water shifts; kidney salt excretion explains whole-body water loss. Mechanism, not direct sea→cell contact.
