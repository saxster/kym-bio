## Section sequence and fidelity decisions

### 6.1 — Separate air movement from cell chemistry

Start with the mistaken equation “breathing=respiration”; ask whether a root or yeast can release usable energy without lungs. Distinguish ventilation (moving air), gas exchange (oxygen/carbon dioxide crossing surfaces), transport, and cellular reactions. Respiration transfers chemical free energy into ATP-supported work and heat; it does not create energy. Glucose is a teaching fuel, not the only fuel. Plants respire in light and dark; green tissues can simultaneously photosynthesise in light.

Do not repeat the plan's literal “every living cell uses oxygen/mitochondria”. Mature mammalian red cells lack mitochondria and rely on glycolysis; many microbes use fermentation or other electron acceptors. Use “living cells need a supply of usable energy” and specify **aerobic** respiration when claiming oxygen use. Lungs do not perform the entire fuel oxidation. Reconnect to Chapter3 active transport and Chapter4 enzyme-catalysed small steps. ATP is recycled, not a substance eaten in place of food.

Prediction/check proposal: can a root respire without moving air? Explain where molecular oxygen can reach it and where ATP is used. This is a reasoning prompt, not a prevalence claim about learners.

### 6.2 — From fuel to membrane gradient to ATP

Teach mechanisms before pathway names: glucose begins to split in cytoplasm; some ATP is made there. Further carbon reactions in a mitochondrion release CO2 and transfer electrons to carriers. Membrane proteins pass these electrons towards O2; released free energy drives pumping H+ **matrix→intermembrane space**. The inner membrane holds a difference in both concentration and electric potential. H+ moving back **intermembrane space→matrix** through synthase drives rotation/shape changes and ATP production from ADP+Pi. Oxygen accepts electrons and protons to form water at the electron chain's end. Carbon dioxide is released at earlier carbon-processing reactions, not by oxygen colliding with intact sugar in the lungs or at the turbine.

Net glucose account: C6H12O6+6O2→6CO2+6H2O. This omits carrier cycling, enzyme steps and ATP/ADP from the material summary. It is not the single-step mechanism and does not claim each O atom of inhaled O2 immediately appears in CO2. Proton movements, electron movements and carbon movements need different arrows/labels. ATP hydrolysis releases free energy through the **whole reaction**, including formation/solvation of more stable products; breaking one isolated bond requires energy. Never explain ATP as a spring that releases energy solely when its bond is broken.

Use Mitchell's problem and the1997 fluorescent pointer observation as history (TRUE, as above); make the experimental reverse direction explicit. Do not claim all ATP comes from synthase. Do not specify universal9,000rpm, proton/ATP count, ATP/glucose yield, efficiency, turnover time, or human daily ATP mass. Historical textbook yields vary with assumptions, tissues and conditions, and are unnecessary to this explanation.

Prediction/check: if the inner membrane becomes freely leaky to H+, can pumping maintain the useful gradient? Distinguish “electron transfer may continue” from “normal ATP yield continues”. Avoid experimental chemical uncouplers at home.

### 6.3 — Follow matter, then distinguish two different mass accounts

The precise subject is **complete oxidation of a representative triglyceride**, not a diet, a kilogram of adipose tissue, instantaneous scale change, or a prescription to lose mass. Use an impersonal specimen/account; no body targets, calories, food restriction or exercise targets. Fat is essential biological material. Exhaling faster by choice does not force fuel oxidation; the mass ledger is not a breathing intervention.

MB14 model: C55H104O6+78O2→55CO2+52H2O. A real mixture contains different triglycerides. Total product masses include incoming oxygen; a1kg starting fuel can therefore produce more than1kg products. Separately, MB14's approximate original-fat allocation puts its carbon and an assigned share of oxygen into CO2 and its hydrogen plus the remaining assigned oxygen into water. The **84%/16%** is that original-fat allocation, not a CO2/water product ratio, nor the mass fraction of carbon alone.

MB14 explains rapid exchange of oxygen between body water and respiratory CO2 via carbonic acid, and assigns four of the fat's six oxygen atoms toCO2 and two to water for its estimate. The net balanced equation alone does not determine that atom-origin allocation. Do not animate six individually tagged fat oxygen atoms on uniquely predetermined paths; call the allocation an approximate accounting convention/model. All fat carbon in this complete-oxidation model reaches CO2. Water joins body water and may leave by several routes at later times.

Prediction/check: do the total products equal just the starting fat? Require the oxygen input on the balance and distinguish totalCO2 from its assigned original-fat contribution. Recall Chapter5: plant carbon enters from air; oxidation can return fuel carbon to air.

### 6.4 — Keep the carrier reusable

Begin with a bottleneck: the supply of oxidised carrier is finite. Glycolysis transfers electrons to NAD+, producing NADH; if it cannot return those electrons, the initial splitting pathway cannot keep running. Fermentation transfers electrons to organic products and restores NAD+, allowing glycolysis to continue. It does not add an extra ATP harvest beyond the two net ATP of the taught glycolysis route.

For yeast: glucose→2ethanol+2CO2; gas can puff dough. For muscles: pyruvate receives electrons to become lactate, regenerating NAD+; the glucose-to-lactate route releases noCO2. The school neutral formula glucose→2lactic-acid molecules is a simplified material summary; intracellular product is mainly lactate. Modern lactate production can occur when oxygen is present, especially during high glycolytic flux. Lactate can be carried and oxidised elsewhere or used to make glucose; do not insist it is all burnt in the liver, is merely waste, or causes delayed soreness. Avoid an oxygen switch that turns all aerobic processing instantly off at exercise onset.

Idli: gas-producing bacterial fermentation is a real Indian example; ID65 identifies L.mesenteroides in its samples. Other batters and starter experiments include different bacteria and yeasts. Sourness, trapped gas and microbial growth are different observations; batter volume alone neither identifies a species nor measures gas production/respiration rate. Do not give the yeast equation as the unique idli equation, guarantee overnight doubling, or assign universal gas volume/time/temperature.

Use “fermentation” for these pathways, and one kind `:::school` bridge that Cambridge groups yeast/muscle examples under **anaerobic respiration**. Scientific anaerobic respiration using an electron chain and other terminal acceptors is a separate concept; a short folded clarification is sufficient. Retain examiner-facing oxygen-debt wording in a clearly labelled bridge, with a modern correction beside it.
