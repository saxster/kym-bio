## Scientific contract and fidelity traps

### Energy and food supply

The Sun is the principal biological energy input. Respiration transfers chemical energy through cellular work and eventually to heat. Waste and uneaten material can retain chemical energy for detrital food webs; being unavailable to the next consumer is not the same as having already become heat. Ecosystems exchange matter with their surroundings; atoms can be reused while their chemical forms change.

Here trophic efficiency is **new biomass production at level n+1 divided by production at level n**, for the same area and interval. Ten percent is an illustrative assumption, not a thermodynamic constant, a universal ingestion/assimilation rate or a measurement of SGNP. A consistently bounded production pyramid is upright. Imported food and different spatial boundaries must be accounted for; a snapshot biomass pyramid is a different measurement and can invert in fast-turnover systems.

The model's respiratory/egested/excreted/uneaten shares are chosen illustrative allocations, not universal measured fractions. Heat + detrital outputs + final consumer production must close the starting energy account without double counting producer detritus. No minimum viable predator threshold can be inferred from kJ per square metre alone: area, consumer requirements, transfer efficiencies and web structure also matter.

Keep food supplied to humans separate. For the diet widget, 10,000 kJ harvested edible crop energy goes either directly into food supply or through one assumed feed-to-edible-animal-food conversion. At 10%, all-direct = 10,000 kJ, all-livestock = 1,000 kJ; 90% crop allocation direct gives 9,100 kJ; 10% gives 1,900 kJ. These are before human digestion. Relative model food energy is not an independently measured people-per-hectare ratio. Grass and by-products not edible by people also matter in real farming comparisons.

### Population growth

Use a microbial batch culture for lag, exponential, stationary and death phases. Few cells alone do not explain a physiological lag: cells acclimatise and synthesise necessary machinery. Resource limitation is initially weak, not literally absent. Stationary density can arise from approximately balanced division/death and/or very slow division. A later death phase is possible when a closed culture deteriorates. Carrying capacity describes sustainable population size under prevailing environmental conditions and changes with those conditions; wild populations need not follow a tidy batch-culture curve. Human numbers rose rapidly, but percentage growth has slowed since the 1960s.

### Carbon, water and nitrogen

Water changes state and location through evaporation, transpiration, condensation, precipitation and drainage. Do not assign an unsupported fixed share of Western Ghats rainfall to transpiration. Photosynthesis is the principal carbon-entry pathway in the sunlit model, not the only biological carbon fixation on Earth: chemoautotrophs also fix inorganic carbon. Organic burial and geological processing can form fossil fuels. Limestone commonly forms through accumulated carbonate shells/skeletons or carbonate precipitation, a separate pathway.

Biological fixation: N₂ → NH₃/NH₄⁺, via nitrogen-fixing microbes. Lightning: nitrogen oxides → nitrate in rain; it does not produce ammonium/amino acids by the root-nodule route. Industrial Haber fixation produces ammonia. Decomposition/ammonification returns organic nitrogen to ammonium. Aerobic nitrification: NH₄⁺ → NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻. Denitrification in oxygen-poor conditions reduces nitrate towards nitrogen gas. Denitrification can remove crop-available nitrogen but can help remove excessive nitrate from polluted waters.

Roots need usable nitrogen and sufficient energy for uptake. Legume nodules supply fixed nitrogen to the plant; higher soil nitrate is not immediate or guaranteed. Plant uptake, residue return, fertiliser, leaching and drainage affect measured nitrate. Avoid inferring Plot B's crop/nodules when its crop is unspecified. Leghaemoglobin buffers free oxygen while supporting respiration. Market plants may lack intact roots or visible nodules. No nodules found is not proof that legumes do not fix nitrogen.

### Nutrient pollution and conservation

A useful common route: nutrient input → producer growth → organic matter → decomposer respiration → reduced dissolved oxygen → harm to oxygen-dependent organisms. Shading of submerged plants can contribute but is not compulsory. Dead algae themselves feed decomposers; sewage organic matter can do so directly. Algae respire by day and night; adequate daylight can make net oxygen production positive, while pre-dawn oxygen can become dangerously low. Nitrate toxicity depends on concentration, exposure and species; teach the intended oxygen-depletion mechanism without asserting that nitrate is harmless under every runoff condition.

BOD is oxygen demand measured over a specified test period, not a count of bacteria. Hypoxic versus anoxic must not be conflated. Species vary in tolerance. The model's oxygen, bloom, survival and nitrogen values are teaching outputs, not calibrated predictions for Powai or Thane Creek.

Many plastics resist environmental breakdown, but polymer chemistry differs: nylon is a polyamide. Distinguish particle ingestion, retention and trophic transfer from increasing concentration up a chain. Plastic-associated chemicals and particle effects require evidence at relevant exposures. Use DDT/DDE as the established persistent-pollutant example; DDT is degraded slowly, sometimes into persistent DDE, not never degraded.

Conservation maintains species diversity, vulnerable ecosystems, nutrient cycling and sources of food, medicines, fuel and genes. Replanting requires seedling survival and time to replace mature biomass/habitat. Suitable seeds tolerate drying/freezing; some species require specialised methods. AI introduces sperm into a female; IVF involves fertilisation outside the body and subsequent embryo transfer. These can connect suitable donors but do not create alleles already lost. Small populations can lose variation and suffer inbreeding depression; linkage drag is a different genetic concept.

### Biotechnology and editing

Some laboratory E. coli can divide every 20 minutes under suitable conditions. One cell reaches about a billion after 30 doublings, or ten idealised hours; this is not all bacteria's growth rate or a promise of unrestricted industrial growth. Bread rises through CO₂. Baking removes much ethanol, but small amounts can remain; oven temperature alone cannot predict residual ethanol. Enzyme optima and heat tolerance depend on the enzyme/formulation.

Fermenter conditions include temperature, pH, dissolved oxygen, nutrients and waste products, supported by asepsis and mixing. An acid or alkali is not automatically a buffer. Cooling removes respiratory heat. Failure can cause rising temperature and falling productivity, but no specified time-to-death or final temperature follows without a heat budget. Optima and denaturation thresholds are organism/process dependent; the 37 °C illustrative response is not a universal microbial curve.

For insulin: prepare **intron-free** synthetic/cDNA coding instructions and bacterial expression controls. Obtain compatible DNA ends (same restriction enzyme in the Cambridge example); ligase seals backbones; transform and select appropriate cells; culture, recover, process/fold and purify the product. Ordinary E. coli cannot simply splice introns from raw human genomic insulin DNA. The nearly universal code explains codon meanings, not expression controls or protein maturation. Mature insulin has two chains, 21 and 30 residues, linked by disulfide bonds. The original recombinant method used separately synthesised instructions for the two chains; other routes use a precursor requiring processing.

Different enzymes can produce compatible ends (BamHI/BglII); EcoRI/BamHI ends are incompatible in the stated ordinary cloning setup. Not every restriction enzyme makes sticky ends. GM can insert one or several genes, delete DNA or edit bases; useful crop development still involves selection/testing and often further breeding. Golden Rice's humanitarian licence allows royalty-free access and seed reuse; do not attach generic annual patented-seed costs to it. CRISPR guide RNAs have a target-matching region within a longer RNA. Cas9 cuts; cellular repair creates sequence changes. A chosen correction is not guaranteed. Approved sickle-cell editing can increase fetal haemoglobin instead of correcting the HbS letter.

## History and place checks

- Elton's *Animal Ecology*: 1927. Lindeman's trophic-dynamic paper: 1942. Do not turn its observations into proof of a universal ten-percent law.
- Keeling measurements began at Mauna Loa in 1958. Distinguish site/year/season from global annual mean when giving a modern concentration.
- Carson's *Silent Spring*: 1962. Separate her public synthesis from later DDE/eggshell-mechanism research.
- Silent Valley dam abandoned in 1983; formal national-park notification dated 15 November 1984.
- Oxford penicillin treatment development involved Chain, Florey, Heatley and others; Peoria research and industry contributed to deep-tank mass production. Do not call a later production strain simply Fleming's original mould.
- HAL incorporated at Pimpri in 1954; plant commissioned 1955–56. SubbaRow died in 1948 and did not launch it in 1954.
- Cohen/Boyer and colleagues' 1973 plasmid demonstration was not the 1978 synthetic insulin-chain experiment. Humulin approved in 1982.
- Biocon uses a Pichia yeast platform for its recombinant human insulin; do not merge it with HAL or call that product an E. coli example.
- Mithi drains into Mahim Creek. Thane Creek is not its downstream restoration station.
