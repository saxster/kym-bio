## 3. Historical, Experimental, and Quantitative Grounding (`TRUE / HEDGE / LEGEND` Discipline)

### 3.1 Gregor Johann Mendel (1822–1884) and the Brno Pea Experiments (1856–1866) (`§13.1`)
- **Primary Source:** Mendel, G. (`1866`), "Versuche über Pflanzen-Hybriden" (*Experiments on Plant Hybrids*), read at the meetings of 8 February and 8 March 1865, published in *Verhandlungen des naturforschenden Vereines in Brünn*, Bd. `IV` (Abhandlungen): 3–47 (`1866`).
- **Biographical & Institutional Grounding (`TRUE`):**
  - Born **Johann Mendel** on `20 July 1822` in Heinzendorf bei Odrau (Hynčice, Austrian Silesia, now Czech Republic) to a peasant farming and orchard-grafting family (his father Anton Mendel and parish priest Johann Schreiber taught him fruit-tree grafting and pomology early in life).
  - Entered the **Augustinian Abbey of St Thomas in Brno (Brünn)** in `1843`, taking the religious name **Gregor**, under Abbot **Cyrill Franz Napp** (`1792–1867`), who actively promoted sheep-breeding and agricultural heredity research in Moravia.
  - Studied experimental physics, combinatorial mathematics, and plant physiology at the **University of Vienna (`1851–1853`)** under **Christian Doppler** (physics), **Andreas von Ettingshausen** (combinatorial analysis and probability), and **Franz Unger** (botany and cell theory). That physics-and-combinatorics training is why Mendel designed large-sample quantitative trials and recognised `1 : 2 : 1` as the binomial expansion `(A + a)² = A² + 2Aa + a²`.
  - Between **`1856` and `1863`**, Mendel cultivated and scored roughly **`28,000` garden pea plants (*Pisum sativum*)** in the abbey's **`35 m × 7 m` (`245 m²`) experimental garden plot** and greenhouse.
- **Why *Pisum sativum* Was the Ideal Experimental Organism (`TRUE`):**
  1. **Closed keel petals (`cleistogamous` self-pollination by default):** In the pea flower, two fused lower petals form a boat-shaped **keel** that completely encloses the pollen-making **anthers** and egg-holding **carpel (stigma + ovules)**, so the flower normally **self-pollinates** before the bud even opens, preventing stray wind or insect pollen contamination.
  2. **Easy artificial cross-pollination:** By opening an immature flower bud before its own pollen ripened, snipping away its anthers with fine forceps (**emasculation**), dusting the stigma with pollen from a chosen parent using a **camel-hair brush**, and covering the flower with a small calico bag, Mendel controlled parentage with `100%` certainty.
  3. **Two-year pure-breeding (`homozygous`) verification (`1854–1856`):** Mendel obtained `34` distinct pea varieties from seed merchants and bred them for two full years before starting his crosses, retaining **`22` pure-breeding varieties** that bred `100%` true generation after generation across **seven sharply contrasting traits**.
- **Exact Published `F₁` and `F₂` Counts Across All Seven Traits (`Mendel 1866`, `TRUE`):**

- **Mendel's `F₃` Progeny Test Revealing the Hidden `1 : 2 : 1` Genotypic Ratio (`TRUE`):**
  - Mendel realised that the `3 : 1` phenotypic ratio in `F₂` was a disguise for a **`1 : 2 : 1` genotypic ratio** (`1` pure-breeding dominant `RR` : `2` hybrid dominant `Rr` : `1` pure-breeding recessive `rr`).
  - To prove this experimentally, he took **`565` plants grown from round `F₂` seeds** and allowed them to self-pollinate into the **`F₃` generation**:
    - **`193` plants (`34.16% ≈ 1/3` of the round `F₂` plants)** produced **only round seeds (`100%` pure-breeding `RR`)**.
    - **`372` plants (`65.84% ≈ 2/3` of the round `F₂` plants)** segregated once again into **`3 round : 1 wrinkled` (`Rr` heterozygotes)** (`372 : 193 = 1.93 : 1 ≈ 2 : 1`).
  - Similarly, for seed cotyledon colour (`519` yellow `F₂` seeds grown to `F₃`), **`166` bred purely yellow (`YY`)** and **`353` segregated `3 yellow : 1 green` (`Yy`)** (`353 : 166 = 2.13 : 1 ≈ 2 : 1`).
- **Historical `TRUE / HEDGE / LEGEND` Audit on Mendel:**
  - **`TRUE` (Terminology):** Mendel himself used the German words *Elemente* or *Faktoren* and coined the terms **dominant** (*dominirende*) and **recessive** (*recessive*) in `1866`. After the simultaneous `1900` rediscovery of Mendel's laws by **Hugo de Vries**, **Carl Correns**, and **Erich von Tschermak**, British biologist **William Bateson (`1861–1926`)**—working with **Edith Rebecca Saunders (`1865–1945`)** and **Reginald Crundall Punnett (`1875–1967`)** at Cambridge—coined the words ***allelomorph* (shortened to *allele*, `1902`)**, ***homozygote* (`1902`)**, ***heterozygote* (`1902`)**, and ***genetics* (`1905`)**, while **Reginald Punnett** introduced the **Punnett square** diagram (`1905–1906`). Danish botanist **Wilhelm Johannsen (`1857–1927`)** coined the words ***gene* (*Gen*)**, ***genotype* (*Genotypus*)**, and ***phenotype* (*Phänotypus*)** in **`1909`**.
  - **`LEGEND` vs `TRUE` (Darwin and Mendel's paper):** It is a widespread textbook legend that Charles Darwin owned an uncut reprint of Mendel's `1866` paper in his study at Down House. Historical cataloguing of Darwin's library at Cambridge and Down House (`Bizzo & El-Hani 2009`; `Fairbanks 2020`) proved that **Darwin never possessed a copy of Mendel's 1866 paper**; what sat uncut in Darwin's library were the pages of W. O. Focke's `1881` reference book *Die Pflanzen-Mischlinge*, which briefly mentioned Mendel on page `109` without explaining his ratios.
  - **`HEDGE` (The Fisher 1936 statistical note):** In `1936`, statistician R. A. Fisher noted that Mendel's aggregate data fit theoretical expectations remarkably closely (`χ²` analysis); subsequent botanical and statistical analyses (`Weiling 1986`; `Fairbanks & Rytting 2001`; `Hartl & Fairbanks 2007`) showed that scoring `10` seeds per `F₂` plant and tetrad pollen biology account naturally for much of the reduced variance without any fabrication.

### 3.2 Molecular Basis of Mendel's Dominant & Recessive Pea Alleles (`§13.1`)
- **Round (`R`) vs Wrinkled (`r`) Seeds — `SBEI` Starch-Branching Enzyme (`TRUE`):**
  - **Primary Source:** Bhattacharyya, M. K., Smith, A. M., Ellis, T. H. N., Hedley, C., & Martin, C. (`1990`), "The wrinkled-seed character of pea described by Mendel is caused by a transposon-like insertion in a gene encoding starch-branching enzyme", *Cell* `60`(1): 115–122.
  - **Mechanism:** The `R` locus on pea Chromosome `5` (linkage group `VII`) encodes **starch-branching enzyme isoform I (`SBEI`)**, which catalyses α-`1,6` glycosidic branch points to convert linear amylose/sugars into branched **amylopectin starch** in developing cotyledons:
    - **`RR` (homozygous dominant):** `2` functional copies (`100%` enzyme activity) → high amylopectin starch, low free sucrose → seed loses water smoothly during maturation → **round seed**.
    - **`Rr` (heterozygous):** `1` functional copy (`R`) + `1` disrupted copy (`r`) → **`~50%` `SBEI` enzyme activity**. Because enzymes are catalytic (`Ch 4`: one enzyme molecule turns over thousands of substrate molecules per second), a `50%` enzyme dose is more than enough (**haplosufficiency**) to synthesise normal branched starch → **round seed** (indistinguishable macroscopically from `RR`).
    - **`rr` (homozygous recessive):** `0` functional copies (`0%` `SBEI` activity, caused by an `0.8 kb` *Ac/Ds*-like transposon insertion into the `SBEI` coding sequence) → starch synthesis stalls, **free sucrose accumulates to nearly double normal levels (`~10%` vs `~5%` dry weight)**, lowering the internal **water potential (`Ch 3`)** so the developing seed absorbs extra water by **osmosis** and swells larger while green; when the seed ripens and dries out, it loses that larger volume of water and the stretched seed coat **collapses into deep wrinkles**.
- **Purple (`B` / `A`) vs White (`b` / `a`) Flowers — Anthocyanin Transcription Factor (`TRUE`):**
  - **Primary Source:** Hellens, R. P. et al. (`2010`), "Identification of Mendel's white flower character", *PLoS ONE* `5`(10): e13230.
  - **Mechanism:** The flower-colour gene encodes a **bHLH transcription factor** that switches on the enzymes synthesising purple **anthocyanin pigment** in petals. One working copy (`Bb`) switches on the pigment pathway (**purple petals**); only when both copies carry a splice-site mutation (`bb`, `G → A` transition creating a premature stop codon) does the pathway stay dark, leaving petals **white**.
- **Tall (`T` / `Le`) vs Dwarf (`t` / `le`) Stems — Gibberellin `3β`-Hydroxylase (`TRUE`):**
  - **Primary Source:** Lester, D. R., Ross, J. J., Aitken, E. A., & Reid, J. B. (`1997`), "Mendel's stem length gene (*Le*) encodes a gibberellin 3β-hydroxylase", *The Plant Cell* `9`(8): 1435–1443.
  - **Mechanism:** A single `G → A` base substitution (`Ala²²⁹ → Thr²²⁹`) near the active site of gibberellin `3β`-hydroxylase drastically reduces conversion of inactive `GA₂₀` into the active growth hormone **`GA₁`**, shortening stem internodes from `~1.8–2.0 m` (`TT` or `Tt`) to `~0.3–0.5 m` (`tt`).

### 3.3 Codominance, `Hbᴬ/Hbˢ`, Karl Landsteiner (1900–1901) & Human ABO Blood Groups (`§13.3`)
- **Codominance Definition (`0610 17.4.10 Supplement`, `TRUE`):**
  - **Codominance** is **a situation in which both alleles in heterozygous organisms contribute to the phenotype**.
  - Unlike complete dominance (`3 : 1` `F₂` phenotypic ratio because `Aa` looks like `AA`), in a codominant cross between two heterozygotes (`CᴿCᵂ × CᴿCᵂ` or `IᴬIᴮ × IᴬIᴮ`), the heterozygote has its own distinct third phenotype, so the **phenotypic ratio (`1 : 2 : 1`) is identical to the genotypic ratio (`1 : 2 : 1`)**.
- **Human ABO Blood Group System (`0610 17.4.11 & 17.4.14 Supplement`, `TRUE`):**
  - **Primary Sources:**
    - Landsteiner, K. (`1900`), "Zur Kenntnis der antifermentativen, lytischen und agglutinierenden Wirkungen des Blutserums und der Lymphe", *Zentralblatt für Bakteriologie* `27`: 357–362; and Landsteiner, K. (`1901`), "Über Agglutinationserscheinungen normalen menschlichen Blutes", *Wiener klinische Wochenschrift* `14`: 1132–1134 (Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine `1930`).
    - Bernstein, F. (`1924`), "Ergebnisse einer biometrischen Untersuchung der erblichen Blutstrukturen des Menschen", *Klinische Wochenschrift* `3`: 1495–1497 (proved that ABO blood groups are inherited via **three multiple alleles `Iᴬ`, `Iᴮ`, and `Iᴼ` at a single genetic locus**, rather than two unlinked loci).
    - Yamamoto, F., Clausen, H., White, T., Marken, J., & Hakomori, S. (`1990`), "Molecular genetic basis of the histo-blood group ABO system", *Nature* `345`: 229–233.
  - **Molecular Glycosyltransferase Mechanism (`ABO` locus on human Chromosome `9q34.2`, `TRUE`):**
    - All normal red blood cells first build a common core oligosaccharide stalk (**H antigen**) on their cell membrane glycoproteins and glycolipids.
    - The `I` (*isoagglutinogen*) gene on **Chromosome `9`** codes for a **glycosyltransferase enzyme** (`354` amino acids) in the Golgi apparatus that attaches one final terminal sugar onto that H-antigen stalk:
      1. **`Iᴬ` allele (`α-1,3-N-acetylgalactosaminyltransferase`):** Attaches **N-acetylgalactosamine (`GalNAc`)** onto the H stalk, forming **Antigen A** on the red blood cell membrane.
      2. **`Iᴮ` allele (`α-1,3-galactosyltransferase`):** Differs from `Iᴬ` at `4` key amino-acid positions (`Arg¹⁷⁶→Gly`, `Gly²³⁵→Ser`, `Leu²⁶⁶→Met`, `Gly²⁶⁸→Ala`) in the substrate-binding pocket, causing the enzyme to attach **D-galactose (`Gal`)** instead, forming **Antigen B** on the red blood cell membrane.
      3. **`Iᴼ` allele (frameshift deletion):** Carries a single-base deletion (`ΔG261` at codon `87`, `Ch 11.4` frameshift) that shifts the reading frame and hits a premature stop codon at residue `117`, producing a **truncated, catalytically inactive protein (`0%` transferase activity)** that adds **neither A nor B sugar** to the H stalk.
  - **Why `Iᴬ` and `Iᴮ` are Codominant while both are Dominant to `Iᴼ` (`0610 17.4.11`):**
    - In **`IᴬIᴮ`** heterozygotes, **both** the `Iᴬ` enzyme and the `Iᴮ` enzyme are active simultaneously in every developing red blood cell, decorating the membrane with **both Antigen A and Antigen B** → phenotype **Blood Group `AB`** (**codominance**).
    - In **`IᴬIᴼ`** (`Group A`) and **`IᴮIᴼ`** (`Group B`) heterozygotes, the single functional `Iᴬ` or `Iᴮ` copy makes plenty of enzyme to coat the membrane with Antigen A or Antigen B, while the inactive `Iᴼ` allele adds nothing → **`Iᴬ` and `Iᴮ` are dominant over the recessive allele `Iᴼ`**.
    - Only **`IᴼIᴼ`** homozygotes have zero A or B transferase activity → **Blood Group `O`**.

Sukumaran** used ABO/MN/Rh blood-group serology, paper electrophoresis of `HbA` and `HbS`, and X-linked colour-vision (`Ishihara`) and `G6PD` pedigrees to map allele frequencies across communities in Mumbai, Maharashtra, and Gujarat—establishing human population genetics and genetic counselling in India.

### 3.4 Sex Determination (`XX` vs `XY`) and Sex-Linked Inheritance (`§13.4`)
- **Nettie Stevens (1905) and Edmund Beecher Wilson (1905) — Discovery of `XX` and `XY` Sex Chromosomes (`TRUE`):**
  - **Primary Sources:** Stevens, N. M. (`1905`), *Studies in Spermatogenesis with Especial Reference to the "Accessory Chromosome"*, Carnegie Institution of Washington Publication No. `36`, Pt. 1: 1–32 (submitted May 1905, published September 1905); Wilson, E. B. (`1905`), "The chromosomes in relation to the determination of sex in insects", *Science* `22`(564): 500–502.
  - Working at **Bryn Mawr College**, cytogeneticist **Nettie Maria Stevens (`1861–1912`)** examined mealworm beetles (*Tenebrio molitor*, `2n = 20`) and discovered that female somatic cells contain `20` large chromosomes (`10` matched pairs, including `XX`) and produce eggs all carrying `10` large chromosomes (`X`), whereas male somatic cells contain `19` large chromosomes plus **`1` tiny chromosome (`Y`)** and produce two equal classes of sperm (`50%` with the large `X` chromosome → female `XX` zygotes; `50%` with the small `Y` chromosome → male `XY` zygotes).
- **Human Karyotype Asymmetry between `X` and `Y` (`0610 17.1 & 17.4.12–17.4.14`, `TRUE`):**
  - Human diploid cells (`2n = 46`) have **22 pairs of autosomes** (`Chromosomes 1–22`, identical in length and gene loci in both sexes) plus **1 pair of sex chromosomes (`Pair 23`)**: **`XX` in females** and **`XY` in males**.
  - **Size and gene-content contrast (`GRCh38 / T2T-CHM13`):**
    - **Human `X` chromosome:** **`~156 million` base pairs (`156 Mb`)**, carrying **`~800–900` protein-coding genes** (`~5%` of the haploid genome), encoding proteins needed in both males and females (retinal red and green cone **opsins** `OPN1LW`/`OPN1MW`, blood-clotting **Factor VIII** `F8` and **Factor IX** `F9`, muscle **dystrophin** `DMD`, and glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase `G6PD`).
    - **Human `Y` chromosome:** **`~57 million` base pairs (`57 Mb`)**, carrying **`< 100` unique protein-coding genes** (around `45–70` distinct gene families, notably the male-determining **`SRY`** gene), and **lacking homologous loci for almost all X-linked genes**.
  - **Why X-linked recessive traits are much more common in males than females (`0610 17.4.12`):**
    - A **sex-linked characteristic** is **a feature in which the gene responsible is located on a sex chromosome and that this makes it more common in one sex than in the other**.
    - Because a male (`XY`) inherits his **single `X` chromosome from his mother** and his **`Y` chromosome from his father**, and the `Y` chromosome has **no locus** for X-linked genes (`Xᴮ/Xᵇ` or `Xᴴ/Xʰ`), a male has **only one allele** for every X-linked gene (**hemizygous**). If that single allele is recessive (`XᵇY` or `XʰY`), there is no dominant allele to mask it, so the recessive phenotype is **always expressed** (frequency in males `= q`).
    - A female (`XX`) inherits **two `X` chromosomes** (one from her mother, one from her father). If she inherits one recessive allele (`Xᵇ` or `Xʰ`) and one normal dominant allele (`Xᴮ` or `Xᴴ`), she is a **heterozygous carrier (`XᴮXᵇ` or `XᴴXʰ`)** with a normal phenotype; she only expresses the recessive condition if she inherits the recessive allele from **both** parents (`XᵇXᵇ` or `XʰXʰ`, frequency `= q² ≪ q`; e.g. if `q ≈ 0.08` (`8%`) of `X` chromosomes carry `Xᵇ` for red–green colour blindness, **`~8%` (`1 in 12`) of males** are colour-blind (`XᵇY`), whereas only **`q² = 0.08² = 0.0064 = 0.64%` (`~1 in 156`) of females** are colour-blind (`XᵇXᵇ`)).
- **Thomas Hunt Morgan (1910) and the White-Eyed *Drosophila* Male (`TRUE`):**
  - **Primary Source:** Morgan, T. H. (`1910`), "Sex limited inheritance in *Drosophila*", *Science* `32`(812): 120–122 (`22 July 1910`; Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine `1933`).
  - Working in Room `613` of Schermerhorn Hall (the **"Fly Room"**) at **Columbia University** in New York, **Thomas Hunt Morgan (`1866–1945`)** bred vinegar fruit flies (*Drosophila melanogaster*, `2n = 8`, generation time `~12 days`) in milk bottles stocked with mashed bananas.
  - In **May `1910`**, Morgan spotted a single **white-eyed male fly (`XʷY`)** among thousands of wild-type **red-eyed (`Xᴿ`)** flies:
    1. **Cross 1 (`P`: Red-eyed female `XᴿXᴿ` × White-eyed male `XʷY`):** `F₁` offspring were **`1,237` red-eyed flies** (`XᴿXʷ` carrier females and `XᴿY` red-eyed males; plus `3` rare exceptional white males).
    2. **Cross 2 (`F₁ × F₁`: Carrier female `XᴿXʷ` × Red-eyed male `XᴿY`):** `F₂` offspring comprised `2,459` red-eyed females, `1,011` red-eyed males, and **`782` white-eyed males**—and **`0` white-eyed females** (`3.475 red : 1 white` overall, with **100% of the white-eyed `F₂` flies being male**).
    3. **Cross 3 (`Test cross`: Carrier female `XᴿXʷ` × White-eyed male `XʷY`):** Produced all four combinations in a `1 : 1 : 1 : 1` ratio (`129` red females `XᴿXʷ`, `132` red males `XᴿY`, `88` white females `XʷXʷ`, `86` white males `XʷY`), proving that the eye-colour gene sits physically on the **`X` chromosome**.
- **Two Mandatory Cambridge `0610 17.4.13` Human X-Linked Examples (`TRUE`):**
  1. **Red–green colour blindness (`Xᴮ` normal vs `Xᵇ` colour-blind):** Caused by recessive mutations/recombinations in the **`OPN1LW` (red, long-wavelength `560 nm`)** or **`OPN1MW` (green, medium-wavelength `530 nm`)** photopigment opsin genes on **`Xq28`** (connecting back to retinal cone cells in Chapter `9.4`).
  2. **Haemophilia A & B (`Xᴴ` normal clotting vs `Xʰ` haemophilia):** Caused by recessive loss-of-function mutations in the **`F8` gene (coagulation Factor VIII)** or **`F9` gene (coagulation Factor IX)** on **`Xq28 / Xq27`** (connecting back to the blood-clotting cascade `fibrinogen → insoluble fibrin mesh` in Chapters `8.4` and `10.4`).
