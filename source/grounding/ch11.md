## 3. Historical & Empirical Grounding (`TRUE / HEDGE / LEGEND` Discipline)

All historical accounts follow `source/research/history.md`, `source/STORYWRITING.md`, and `source/provenance/sources.yml`:
1. **Frederick Griffith (1928) & Oswald Avery, Colin MacLeod, and Maclyn McCarty (1944):**
   - *TRUE:* Griffith (*J. Hyg.*, 1928) showed that heat-killed virulent smooth (**S**, capsulated) *Streptococcus pneumoniae* (then *Diplococcus pneumoniae*) transformed living non-virulent rough (**R**, unencapsulated) bacteria into living virulent **S** bacteria whose descendants inherited the smooth capsule.
   - *TRUE:* Avery, MacLeod, and McCarty (*J. Exp. Med.*, 1944) purified the cell-free transforming extract from heat-killed **S** cells and tested enzymatic destruction systematically:
     - **Proteases** (trypsin and chymotrypsin) destroyed protein → **transformation still occurred** (living **S** cells recovered).
     - **Ribonuclease (RNase)** destroyed RNA → **transformation still occurred**.
     - **Deoxyribonuclease (DNase)** depolymerised DNA → **transformation completely abolished** (only **R** cells recovered).
     - Elemental chemical analysis (`C, H, N, P` ratio, phosphorus-to-nitrogen ratio) and ultraviolet absorption matched pure sodium deoxyribonucleate.
   - *TRUE:* Alfred Hershey and Martha Chase (*J. Gen. Physiol.*, 1952) independently confirmed DNA as the hereditary material in bacteriophage **T2** by labelling phage protein coats with radioactive sulfur (`³⁵S`, present in methionine/cysteine but absent from DNA) and phage DNA with radioactive phosphorus (`³²P`, present in the DNA sugar–phosphate backbone but absent from protein). After blender shearing and centrifugation, ~80% of `³⁵S` stayed outside in the supernatant while `³²P` entered the bacterial pellet and appeared in progeny phages.
2. **Erwin Chargaff (1950), Rosalind Franklin & Raymond Gosling (1952–1953), and James Watson & Francis Crick (1953):**
   - *TRUE:* Erwin Chargaff and colleagues (*Experientia*, 1950) separated hydrolysed DNA bases by paper chromatography and ultraviolet spectrophotometry across diverse species (human thymus/sperm, ox thymus, salmon sperm, sea urchin, yeast, *E. coli*, avian tubercle bacillus). In every double-stranded cellular DNA sample, total adenine approximately equalled thymine (`%A ≈ %T`, `A/T ≈ 1.00`) and total guanine approximately equalled cytosine (`%G ≈ %C`, `G/C ≈ 1.00`), whereas the `(A + T) / (G + C)` ratio varied markedly between species (e.g., human thymus ~`30.9% A, 29.4% T, 19.9% G, 19.8% C`; *E. coli* ~`24.7% A, 23.6% T, 26.0% G, 25.7% C`; *Sarcina lutea* GC-rich). This disproved Phoebus Levene's monotonous "tetranucleotide hypothesis" (which had claimed equal 25% of all four bases in every organism and therefore dismissed DNA as too repetitive to store genetic information).
   - *TRUE:* At King's College London in May 1952, **Rosalind Franklin** and doctoral student **Raymond Gosling** used fine-focus X-ray diffraction and controlled relative humidity (separating the crystalline dry **A-form** from the wet hydrated paracrystalline **B-form** at ~92% humidity) to record a 62-hour exposure of a single sodium thymonucleate fibre (**Photo 51**, published in *Nature*, 25 April 1953, 171:740–741).
   - *TRUE:* Photo 51's stark X-shaped cross of diffraction spots proved a **helical geometry**; the missing fourth layer line reflected the offset between the two coaxial sugar–phosphate chains (generating a major groove and a minor groove); the layer-line spacing gave one full helical turn every **`3.4 nm` (`34 Å`)** and the strong meridional reflection on the tenth layer line gave a vertical step of **`0.34 nm` (`3.4 Å`)** between stacked base pairs (**`10` base pairs per turn**), with a uniform cylinder diameter of **`2.0 nm` (`20 Å`)** and the hydrophilic phosphate groups on the outside exposed to water.
   - *TRUE:* In early 1953, **Maurice Wilkins** showed Photo 51 to **James Watson**, and **Max Perutz** shared a Medical Research Council (MRC) unit report summarizing Franklin's quantitative crystallographic measurements (`C2` monoclinic space group symmetry implying two antiparallel chains, plus her exact unit-cell dimensions) with **Francis Crick** and Watson at the Cavendish Laboratory, Cambridge, without Franklin's prior permission.
   - *TRUE:* After **Jerry Donohue** pointed out that the bases in living cells exist in the **keto** (rather than enol) tautomeric form, Watson and Crick (*Nature*, 25 April 1953, 171:737–738) realised that a two-ring **purine** (`A` or `G`) paired with a one-ring **pyrimidine** (`T` or `C`)—specifically **`A=T` (`2` hydrogen bonds)** and **`C≡G` (`3` hydrogen bonds)**—produces rungs of identical width (`2.0 nm` across the double helix), explains Chargaff's `A = T` and `G = C` ratios, leaves the sequence of bases along a single strand completely free to carry genetic information, and immediately suggests a copying mechanism because each strand serves as a complementary template for the other.
   - *LEGEND Guardrail:* Never write that Franklin "did not understand her own data" or "failed to see that B-DNA was a helix" (her notebooks and draft manuscripts already analysed the B-form helical parameters and outside-phosphate geometry). State the provenance of how her data reached Cambridge factually and without melodrama or invented dialogue.
3. **Francis Crick, Sydney Brenner, Leslie Barnett & R. J. Watts-Tobin (1961), Marshall Nirenberg & Heinrich Matthaei (1961), and Har Gobind Khorana (1964–1968):**
   - *TRUE:* A 4-letter alphabet (`A, U, C, G`) taken 1 letter at a time yields `4¹ = 4` combinations (< 20 amino acids); taken 2 letters at a time yields `4² = 16` combinations (< 20 amino acids); taken **3 letters at a time (a triplet / codon)** yields **`4³ = 64` combinations**, enough for all 20 amino acids plus punctuation, making the code **degenerate** (redundant: most amino acids are specified by 2, 3, 4, or 6 synonymous codons).
   - *TRUE:* Crick, Brenner, Barnett, and Watts-Tobin (*Nature*, 30 December 1961, 192:1227–1232) used acridine (proflavin) insertions (`+`) and deletions (`−`) in the `rII` B cistron of bacteriophage T4 to prove that the genetic code is read in **non-overlapping triplets from a fixed starting point without commas**: `+1` or `+2` (or `−1` or `−2`) shifts the reading frame downstream and scrambles every subsequent amino acid, whereas **`+3` (or `−3`) restores the reading frame** downstream of the three mutations, restoring functional phage protein.
   - *TRUE:* In May 1961 at the NIH in Bethesda, **Marshall Nirenberg** and **J. Heinrich Matthaei** (*PNAS*, 1961) added synthetic polyuridylic acid (**`poly-U`**, `UUUUUU...`) to a cell-free *E. coli* extract containing ribosomes, tRNAs, energy (`ATP/GTP`), and amino acids (with DNase destroying endogenous DNA). The ribosomes synthesised **polyphenylalanine (`Phe–Phe–Phe...`)**, cracking the first codon: **`UUU = Phenylalanine (`Phe`)`**.
   - *TRUE:* Random polynucleotides could not determine the **order of letters** inside mixed triplets (e.g. distinguishing `UCU` from `CUU` or `UUC`). **Har Gobind Khorana** (born in Raipur, Punjab in 1922; studied at Punjab University in Lahore before working in Liverpool, Cambridge, Vancouver, and the University of Wisconsin–Madison) combined organic chemical synthesis of short defined deoxyribooligonucleotides with enzymatic polymerisation (DNA polymerase + RNA polymerase) to create long **repeating copolymers of exact known sequence**:
     - Repeating dinucleotide **`(UC)ₙ = UCUCUCUCUCUC...`** is read in triplets as alternating **`UCU | CUC | UCU | CUC`**, directing ribosomes to synthesise strictly alternating **Serine–Leucine–Serine–Leucine (`Ser–Leu–Ser–Leu`)**.
     - Repeating dinucleotide **`(AG)ₙ = AGAGAGAGAGAG...`** (`AGA | GAG`) produces alternating **Arginine–Glutamic acid (`Arg–Glu–Arg–Glu`)**.
     - Repeating dinucleotide **`(UG)ₙ = UGUGUGUGUGUG...`** (`UGU | GUG`) produces alternating **Cysteine–Valine (`Cys–Val–Cys–Val`)**.
     - Repeatingdinucleotide **`(AC)ₙ = ACACACACACAC...`** (`ACA | CAC`) produces alternating **Threonine–Histidine (`Thr–His–Thr–His`)**.
     - Repeating trinucleotides such as **`(UUC)ₙ = UUCUUCUUC...`** produce three homopolypeptides depending on the starting frame (`UUC → poly-Phe`, `UCU → poly-Ser`, `CUU → poly-Leu`), and **`(UAG)ₙ`** helped confirm that **`UAG`, `UAA`, and `UGA`** are **nonsense (stop) codons** that terminate translation—converging directly with **Obaid Siddiqi**'s and Alan Garen's genetic discovery of nonsense suppressors (Chapter 9).
     - Khorana, Nirenberg, and Robert Holley (who determined the cloverleaf structure of tRNA) shared the 1968 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.
4. **Vernon Ingram (1956–1957) & the Molecular Defect in Beta-Globin (`HBB`):**
   - *TRUE:* Following Linus Pauling, Harvey Itano, S. J. Singer, and Ibert Wells (1949), who showed by electrophoresis that sickle-cell haemoglobin (`HbS`) carries a different electrical charge from normal adult haemoglobin (`HbA`), **Vernon Ingram** at the Cavendish Laboratory (*Nature*, 1956, 178:792–794; *Nature*, 1957, 180:326–328) digested `HbA` and `HbS` with trypsin, separated the resulting peptides by two-dimensional paper electrophoresis and chromatography ("peptide fingerprinting"), and sequenced the single altered peptide (**peptide 4** at the N-terminus of the 146-amino-acid beta-globin chain).
   - *TRUE:* Out of 146 amino acids in the mature beta-globin polypeptide (`HBB` codons 1–7 after cleavage of the initiator methionine: **`Val¹–His²–Leu³–Thr⁴–Pro⁵–Glu⁶–Glu⁷`**, coded by DNA coding strand **`GTG CAC CTG ACT CCT GAG GAG`** / template strand `CAC GTG GAC TGA GGA CTC CTC` / mRNA **`GUG CAC CUG ACU CCU GAG GAG`**), only **position 6 (`Codon 6`)** differs: a single base substitution changes coding DNA **`GAG → GTG`** (template `CTC → CAC`, mRNA **`GAG → GUG`**), replacing negatively charged, hydrophilic **glutamic acid (`Glu`)** with neutral, hydrophobic **valine (`Val`)**.
   - *TRUE:* That single hydrophobic valine creates a sticky non-polar patch on the outside of deoxygenated `HbS` tetramers, causing them to polymerise into rigid fibres that distort flexible biconcave red blood cells into stiff crescent (sickle) shapes.

     - **Step 2:** A single-stranded **messenger RNA (`mRNA`) copy of the gene** is made in the nucleus (`U` replaces `T`: coding DNA `ATG GTG CAC CTG...` → mRNA `AUG GUG CAC CUG...`).
     - **Step 3:** The **mRNA molecule moves out of the nucleus** through a nuclear pore into the **cytoplasm**.
     - **Step 4:** The **mRNA passes through a ribosome** three bases (one codon) at a time.
     - **Step 5:** Transfer RNA (`tRNA`) carriers bring specific amino acids, and the **ribosome links the amino acids via peptide bonds into a protein molecule**.
     - **Step 6:** The **specific sequence of amino acids is determined by the sequence of bases in the mRNA** (and therefore by the sequence of bases in the DNA gene).

7. **`c11_beta_globin_mutation` (`#fig-11-7`):**
   - Single-codon zoom into human beta-globin (`HBB`, codons 1–7: `GTG CAC CTG ACT CCT GAG GAG`):
     - **Normal Adult Beta-Globin (`HbA`):** Codon 6 coding DNA **`GAG`** (template `CTC`) → mRNA **`GAG`** → **Glutamic acid (`Glu⁶`)**, negatively charged and **hydrophilic** (water-loving) → smooth soluble tetramer surface → flexible biconcave red blood cell even at low oxygen.

8. **`c11_mutation_outcomes_comparison` (`#fig-11-8`):**
   - Three-column molecular comparison of single-base substitutions in a coding gene:
     - **(A) Silent (Synonymous) Substitution:** e.g. Beta-globin Codon 3 coding DNA **`CTG → CTT`** → mRNA **`CUG → CUU`**. Because the 64-codon genetic code is degenerate (`CUG` and `CUU` both code for **Leucine, `Leu³`**), the amino-acid sequence (`Val–His–Leu–Thr–Pro–Glu–Glu`) and 3D folded protein shape are **100% unchanged**.
     - **(B) Missense Substitution:** e.g. Beta-globin Codon 6 coding DNA **`GAG → GTG`** → mRNA **`GAG → GUG`** → **`Glu⁶ → Val⁶`**. One amino acid changes, altering local charge/hydrophobicity and 3D surface interactions.
     - **(C) Nonsense Substitution:** e.g. Beta-globin Codon 39 coding DNA **`CAG → TAG`** → mRNA **`CAG` (`Gln`) → `UAG` (`STOP`)**. The ribosome halts prematurely, releasing a truncated, non-functional polypeptide fragment (connecting directly to Obaid Siddiqi's nonsense codon discoveries).
9. **`c11_leucine_silent_case` (`#fig-11-9`):**

     - **Child Y:** Single-base change at **Codon 6 (`GAG → GTG`)** → mRNA `GAG → GUG` → **`Glu⁶ → Val⁶`** (missense) → hydrophobic patch on deoxygenated `HbS`.
