## 2.1 Mechanisms and teaching sequence

Start with a white onion bulb and a green leaf, both plant tissues. Compare observations before naming compartments. A lipid double layer with embedded proteins separates internal chemistry from surroundings and controls passage: cell membrane. Plant cellulose wall outside it provides support; bacterial walls usually contain peptidoglycan, not cellulose. A wall cannot replace a membrane. Cytoplasm includes aqueous material, many molecules and structures; our empty spaces are drawing conventions. Most ordinary plant and animal cells hold most DNA in a membrane-enclosed nucleus: eukaryotic cells (Greek eu, true; karyon, kernel). Bacteria lack a membrane-bound nucleus: prokaryotic cells (pro, before; not “before life”). They have cytoplasm, ribosomes and DNA; school diagram uses a circular chromosome and possible smaller plasmid rings. Not every bacterium carries plasmids, and rare bacterial exceptions to chromosome shape are outside the typical model.

Ribosomes assemble amino acids into proteins from RNA instructions. Mitochondria use chemical energy from nutrients in aerobic respiration to make ATP available for cellular work. Plants have these too, including ordinary onion bulb and root cells: respiration continues in light and darkness. Chloroplasts use light energy to build carbohydrates; white bulb epidermis lacks developed chloroplasts, unlike green photosynthetic leaf cells. Avoid “all plant cells”: not every living cell retains the standard full inventory. Many mature plant cells have a large sap-filled vacuole which helps support the cell through water pressure against the wall; Chapter 3 explains osmosis.

Measured source ranges (4.2): typical prokaryotes 0.1–5 µm across, typical eukaryotes 10–100 µm, with exceptions. RBC diameter 7–8 µm (40.2). Palisade cell about 50 µm tall in 30.4 figure description. Worked magnification uses an explicitly imagined drawing, not a historical measurement: 50 µm actual, 40 mm image, hence 800×; image resizing changes magnification, so a scale bar travels with the image. Increasing enlargement alone does not improve resolution (4.1). Nominal sizes in the existing explorer are examples, not species-wide constants.

Prediction: will an onion bulb cell without green bodies also lack mitochondria? Evidence: leaf and onion microscopy plus finer structural microscopy; ordinary light microscopy cannot establish absence of tiny ribosomes or resolve membrane bilayers. Hooke's cork (1665, research/history.md; 4.1) showed compartments left by dead cells. It does not establish the full contents of a living cell. Do not call a schematic a micrograph.

## 2.2 Mechanism, history and fidelity

Observed clues: mitochondria and chloroplasts possess DNA, bacterial-related ribosomes, divide from existing organelles and have two surrounding membranes. Comparative gene sequences place mitochondrial ancestry with bacteria (alphaproteobacterial relationship) and chloroplast ancestry with cyanobacteria (23.1). Together these support descent from internal bacterial partners, followed by gene transfer to the host nucleus and dependence; organelles today cannot simply be released as independent bacteria. Two membranes alone cannot prove ancestry. No intention or agreement between organisms, no claim that all organelles originated this way.

TRUE: Lynn Margulis (1938–2011) published `On the origin of mitosing cells` under the name Lynn Sagan in 1967: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11541392/ (doi:10.1016/0022-5193(67)90079-3). She developed an older idea using evidence; she did not single-handedly “prove” it in a single 1967 experiment. Omit the poorly sourced journal-rejection count. Scene: Boston, 1967, scientific work, with no invented room, dialogue or emotions.

TRUE decisive laboratory observation: Hans Ris and Walter Plaut (1962), chloroplast of Chlamydomonas, electron microscopy and cytochemistry; DNA-positive regions and DNase-sensitive fibrils. Primary record https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14492436/ ; doi:10.1083/jcb.13.3.383. Full PMC request hit CAPTCHA; evidence details checked against publisher historical account https://rupress.org/jcb/article-abstract/168/6/853/51523/There-s-DNA-in-those-organelles and indexed original abstract. Describe DNA staining/enzyme comparison simply; do not claim this alone proves endosymbiosis. No unsafe household DNase or microbial culturing activity.

Etymology: endo = within; symbiosis = living together. “Power station” must follow ATP mechanism and must never imply creation of energy. Chloroplast acquisition in the main plant lineage followed mitochondrial ancestry; the plate is a simplified reconstruction without a precise date or exact membrane-origin claim.

## 2.3 Recomputed model and honest kitchen activity

For cube side a, six square faces give SA = 6a²; V = a³; SA/V = 6/a. Units matter: cm²/cm³ = cm⁻¹. Model cubes in centimetres illustrate geometry, not literal cell sizes. For a = 1, 2, 10 cm respectively: SA = 6, 24, 600 cm²; V = 1, 8, 1000 cm³; SA/V = 6, 3, 0.6 cm⁻¹. Tenfold side means 100-fold surface, 1000-fold interior and tenfold lower ratio. Cutting a 2 cm cube into eight 1 cm cubes preserves 8 cm³ while increasing exposed area 24 → 48 cm², if all faces contact solution.

Equal hypothetical penetration depth d gives untouched core max(a−2d, 0)³ and reached fraction 1 − core/a³. At illustrative d = 0.2 cm (2 mm): 1 cm cube reached 78.4%; 2 cm 48.8%; 10 cm 11.5264% (display 11.5%). These are computed geometric fractions, not measured dye, oxygen, uptake or starvation. Three.js slicer and Canvas account must use the same formula. d is manually selected, never a clock or fitted diffusion rate; real gradients are smooth, not sharp-front shells. Expose core by a manual slice control, with 2D equivalent available throughout.

Kitchen trial: adult cuts raw potato 0.5, 1, 2 cm cubes; cool beetroot juice or food colouring, equal liquid conditions and exposure, record time, rinse/blot surface and adult cuts through centre. Inspect at 15 minutes; if no clear edge, record this and compare later or use the model with measured depth if possible. Neither turmeric suspension nor raw-potato tissue guarantees a clean diffusion front; do not promise a 2 mm layer or fully dyed 0.5 cm cube. Dye transport includes tissue permeability, binding and damaged cut cells; it is an analogy, not proof of membrane transport. No eating used food, no hot liquid, dispose same day, wash hands/surfaces; no culturing in a warm flat.

Counterexample case: Thiomargarita namibiensis, coastal shelf sediment off Namibia (not “deep sea” generically), collected 1997, described 1999. Up to 750 µm diameter, a large nitrate-storing central vacuole leaving a thin active cytoplasmic shell: https://www.whoi.edu/press-room/news-release/giant-sulfur-bacteria-discovered-off-african-coast/ ; vacuole evidence and primary-paper pointer https://bionumbers.hms.harvard.edu/bionumber.aspx?id=110079&s=n&v=1 . No current “largest bacterium” claim. Geometry constrains exchange per active volume, not an absolute maximum cell dimension. Long thin neurons, flattened/folded cells, intracellular transport, big vacuoles are workarounds.

## 2.4 Structure → physical job → organisation

RBC (human mature): biconcave, short diffusion distance, flexible, haemoglobin carries oxygen, no nucleus or mitochondria; ATP can still be supplied without mitochondria. This corrects an overgeneral “every animal cell has a nucleus/mitochondria”. Root hair: extension increases contact area with soil water for absorption of water and mineral ions; one cell, not a whole root. Palisade: many chloroplasts and column-like arrangement in leaf for light capture/photosynthesis; has mitochondria too. Ciliated epithelial cells: ATP-driven beating hairs move mucus towards throat in trachea/bronchi; mucus traps particles, not cilia “absorbing” them. Neurone: long narrow processes conduct electrical impulses and branches connect to other cells; no claim that an impulse transports cytoplasm bodily along the axon. School reproduction pair also required: sperm has a swimming tail, egg large cytoplasmic nutrient store, both carry hereditary information; detailed fertilisation deferred.

Cell → tissue (cells working together in a shared function) → organ (different tissues working together) → organ system (organs working together) → organism. Example palisade cell → palisade mesophyll tissue → leaf → shoot system → bean plant. Some tissues include more than one cell type, so avoid defining them as necessarily identical cells. All blood is a tissue; one RBC is not a tissue. Daughter cells come from existing cells by division; larger bodies generally have more cells, not merely enlarged copies of every cell.

Opening estimate: about 30 trillion human cells is an adult reference estimate, not a count of Kym. Sender, Fuchs & Milo (2016), 70 kg reference male: https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.1002533 . Do not state this as an exact universal count.
