# Chapter 10 grounding: regulation, excretion and immunity

Reconciled 1 November 2026 against the independent scientific and editorial reviews in `research/ch06-10-independent-science-review-2026-11-01.md` and `research/ch06-10-editorial-review-2026-11-01.md`. This note replaces the earlier quantitative contracts, whose renal threshold/Tm calculation and immunity guarantees were incorrect. Historical PASS records describe earlier builds and do not validate those premises.

## 1. Teaching sequence and fidelity requirements

The chapter concludes Volume III. It applies Chapter 9's signals to glucose and temperature regulation, then connects Chapter 7's membrane transport to kidney recovery and cholera rehydration. The central task is maintaining and defending an internal environment. Immune memory should not be presented as the same kind of thermostat loop as temperature regulation.

### 10.1 Glucose feedback

Teach the role before alpha/beta terminology. A rise in glucose stimulates pancreatic insulin secretion. Insulin promotes glucose uptake in insulin-sensitive tissues such as skeletal muscle, promotes storage, and suppresses hepatic glucose production. Liver glucose entry is not simply the same insulin-gated process as muscle uptake. A fall in glucose favours glucagon action on liver glycogen breakdown and glucose production. Hormones signal cells to change reactions; they are not enzymes directly converting glucose and glycogen. Glycogen greatly reduces the osmotic burden relative to its constituent glucose units stored free; avoid an absolute claim of zero osmotic effect.

A set point is an illustrative target or range, not an exact invariant. Negative feedback opposes deviation; it need not always oscillate. Type 1 diabetes involves inadequate insulin after beta-cell destruction. Treatment and monitoring descriptions are educational, not an individual prescription. Banting and Best began Toronto experiments in May 1921; Collip joined in December, with purified extract used for Leonard Thompson in January 1922.

### 10.2 Heat balance

Distinguish core temperature, skin temperature, air temperature and surrounding surface temperatures. Convection depends on air–skin difference; net radiation depends on surrounding surfaces, so hot air alone does not determine radiation direction. Sweat evaporation removes about 2,420 J/g in the warm-skin illustration. Dripping is inefficient compared with evaporation; do not describe all non-evaporative heat transfer as literally zero. Arterioles regulate flow to fixed surface capillaries. Sparse human body hair provides little practical insulation compared with furry mammals.

A chosen evaporation rate and heat input define a model scenario, not a physiological prediction determined by relative humidity alone. The Stull approximation gives about 21.34 °C at 38 °C/20% RH and 34.81 °C at 38 °C/80% RH. These are psychrometric illustrations, not universal human tolerance thresholds. Heat damage varies with exposure duration, tissue and protein; no 40–42 °C switch permanently denatures every human enzyme. A kitchen comparison needs equal starting temperatures, measured changes and an honest variable outcome. It has not been physically trialled for this revision.

### 10.3 Kidney paths and quantitative ledger

Keep blood and filtrate as separate routes. Glucose normally filters into the capsule, then nearly all is recovered along the proximal tubule. A two-stage filter-and-recover account is a first model; tubules also secrete substances, and urea is partly reabsorbed and recycled. Blood cells and large proteins are strongly retained; normal urine is not literally free of every protein molecule. A negative dipstick or a rounded model zero cannot establish perfect glomerular integrity. Total plasma protein is distinct from albumin.

ADH is made by hypothalamic cells and released through the posterior pituitary. It increases collecting-duct water permeability through aquaporin regulation. ATP-supported sodium gradients power coupled glucose uptake, reusing the principle from intestinal absorption. Excess nitrogen is processed in the liver and carried as urea towards kidneys. Carbon skeleton is the appropriate term for the remaining amino-acid material. Lack of a dedicated protein store does not imply every spare amino acid must immediately be destroyed.

Independent arithmetic:

- GFR 125 mL/min × 1,440 min/day = 180 L/day. This is an illustrative adult filtration rate, not urine output.
- 5 mmol glucose/L × 180 g/mol = 0.90 g/L = 0.09 g/100 mL. At 180 L/day this filters 162 g glucose/day.
- **Tm is a transport rate**, in mg/min or mmol/day, not a plasma concentration. The plasma threshold for urinary glucose is a different quantity and varies with nephron properties and filtration. Original human data report these separately.
- The revised live model chooses an explicitly illustrative capacity of 330 mg/min. At 16 mmol/L and 125 mL/min, filtered glucose is 360 mg/min. A hard-cap teaching model then leaves 30 mg/min = 43.2 g/day = 240 mmol/day. This is a transparent simplified rate account, not a general clinical threshold curve.
- Excretion rate = filtration rate − reabsorption rate, with secretion included where relevant. Urine concentration requires division by final urine volume. Never subtract a concentration called Tm from plasma glucose and carry that difference unchanged through water reabsorption.
- 180 L filtrate with 178.5 L water reabsorbed gives 1.5 L urine, or 99.17% water recovery. At 0.03 g urea/100 mL, filtered urea is 54 g/day. At 2 g/100 mL and 1.5 L/day, urinary urea is 30 g/day. The difference is accounted by net urea reabsorption, not disappearance.
- In the clinical reasoning case, urine volume is a stipulated observation. Plasma glucose and a transporter capacity alone do not prove a volume of 3.8 L/day. No fatal thirty-minute course follows from loss of glucose reabsorption alone; substantial inherited glucosuria exists.

A coarse kitchen sieve passing salt and sugar demonstrates only its own permeability. Molecular mass is not pore size, and a smaller solute passing a filter cannot prove every larger solute must pass. The activity should model recovery explicitly with labelled counters and a stated selective rule.

### 10.4 Defence, memory and rehydration

Separate transmission, barriers, innate responses and specific adaptive responses. Use neutrophils as the familiar lobed-nucleus phagocyte example; not all phagocytes have that nucleus. B lymphocytes can become antibody-secreting plasma cells; T lymphocytes have other functions. Antigens can be shared or cross-reactive. Antibody binding can neutralise or facilitate other clearance mechanisms; agglutination does not itself mean killing.

Memory can support a faster, stronger response and reduce disease risk or severity. Duration and protection vary. Neither active immunity nor vaccination guarantees lifelong protection or prevention of every infection, symptom or onward transmission. Live attenuated organisms can replicate; killed organisms cannot. A two-antigen comparison that assigns no binding must explicitly assume no relevant cross-recognition. The Ranadive example itself demonstrates cross-recognition between organisms.

Receiving ready-made antibodies does not itself supply the recipient with antigen-specific memory. Placental IgG enters fetal blood; breast-milk antibodies principally protect mucosal surfaces. A 21-day half-life is an illustrative IgG-like model, not one persistence law for every transferred antibody or route.

Cholera toxin alters epithelial signalling to increase chloride secretion; water movement follows the resulting gradients. Sodium–glucose uptake remains available, so a carefully formulated electrolyte–glucose solution supports absorption even while secretion continues. ORT is therapy; ORS is the solution. Describe standard packets prepared to their directions, not pinches or an uncalibrated spoonful. Severe dehydration can require intravenous fluid and clinical care. ORS does not prove the bacteria or toxin have disappeared.

De's 1959 culture-filtrate experiment established a secreted factor causing fluid secretion; do not credit it with direct discovery of every later molecular step. Mahalanabis and colleagues' 1973 Bongaon report describes 3,703 admissions of mixed severity and treatments and an overall 3.6% fatality ratio. Do not label every case severe or imply a controlled identical-group fall from 30% caused by oral treatment alone. Rotavac 116E is a human–bovine natural reassortant; the 2014 trial measures efficacy, not a later annual national impact estimate.

The nine static plates and three interactives must use the corrected mechanisms above. Retain explicit compartment labels, accessible text, figure scale qualifications and manual opt-in controls. Rate ledgers must balance filtered, recovered and excreted amounts. No normal physiological zero or model curve establishes a diagnosis. Thermal response curves are illustrative, with no fixed universal denaturation boundary. Antibody graphs use arbitrary illustrative units and cannot establish complete clinical protection.
