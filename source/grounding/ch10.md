### 1.2 Approved Four-Section Narrative Spine
1. **`10.1` The thermostat and the pantry: homeostasis, negative feedback, and blood glucose**
   - *Scene:* Eating sweet *aamras* (or a ripe Alphonso mango) after an afternoon fast on a hot May day in Mumbai. Why doesn't blood glucose spike to syrup after the mango or drop to zero during an overnight fast?
   - *Core concepts:* **Homeostasis** (`the maintenance of a constant internal environment`, or keeping internal conditions within narrow limits around a **set point** despite external or metabolic changes); **Claude Bernard** (`milieu intérieur`, 1859/1865) and **Walter B. Cannon** (*homeostasis*, 1926/1929); the four-part engineering architecture of **negative feedback** (`stimulus` → `receptor/sensor` → `control centre` → `effector` → `corrective response that opposes the change and restores the set point`); why negative feedback produces small oscillations around the set point (`~4.0–5.5 mmol/L` or `~72–99 mg/dL` fasting blood glucose) and why **positive feedback** amplifies change rather than maintaining homeostasis; **hepatic portal vein** delivering absorbed glucose from the small intestine to the **liver**; **islets of Langerhans** in the **pancreas** acting as both receptor and endocrine coordinator (**beta cells** secreting **insulin** when blood glucose rises above the set point; **alpha cells** secreting **glucagon** when blood glucose falls below the set point); target actions on liver and muscle cells (**insulin** increases membrane glucose uptake via GLUT4/transporters, stimulates respiration of glucose, and activates enzymes that convert soluble **glucose** into insoluble storage **glycogen**; **glucagon** activates liver enzymes that break down stored **glycogen** back into **glucose** released into blood); **Type 1 diabetes** (autoimmune loss of pancreatic insulin-secreting beta cells, persistent hyperglycaemia, glycosuria, osmotic thirst/polyuria, weight loss, and treatment with blood glucose testing, regular **insulin injections**—never tablets because stomach pepsin and intestinal proteases would digest the peptide hormone insulin—and controlled carbohydrate intake and exercise); **Banting, Best, Macleod, and Collip (1921–1922)** and Leonard Thompson.
   - *Plate:* `c10_negative_feedback_glucose()` (`#fig-10-1`).
2. **`10.2` 38 °C in Dadar: skin structure, evaporative cooling, and thermoregulation**
   - *Scene:* Waiting at a Dadar bus stop in May (`38 °C`, `65%` relative humidity) versus walking into an air-conditioned (`18 °C`) shop or stepping out in July monsoon humidity (`30 °C`, `85%` relative humidity). Why must human core temperature stay near `37 °C` even when the street is hotter than your blood?
   - *Core concepts:* Enzyme kinetics and denaturation (`Chapter 4`: below `37 °C`, lower average kinetic energy means fewer frequent/energetic enzyme–substrate collisions; above `40–42 °C`, thermal agitation breaks hydrogen and ionic bonds holding enzyme active sites in their complementary 3D shape, causing irreversible **denaturation**); structure of mammalian **skin** (**epidermis**, **dermis**, **sweat glands** with sweat ducts and pores, **hairs** and **hair erector muscles**, **temperature/touch/pressure receptors** and **sensory neurones**, **arterioles**, **capillary loops**, **venules**, **shunt vessels**, and subcutaneous **fatty/adipose tissue**); coordination by the **brain (hypothalamus)** combining deep blood-temperature receptors with skin temperature receptors; **cooling responses when too hot** (**vasodilation** of smooth muscle in **arterioles** supplying skin surface capillaries so more warm blood flows through surface capillary loops to lose thermal energy by radiation, conduction, and convection when air is cooler than skin, plus **sweat glands** secreting sweat onto the epidermis so water **evaporates**, absorbing the **latent heat of vaporisation** `~2,420 J/g` from the skin, with hair erector muscles relaxed so hairs lie flat); **warming/heat-conservation responses when too cold** (**vasoconstriction** of **arterioles** supplying skin surface capillaries so less blood flows near the surface, **shivering**—rapid involuntary skeletal muscle contractions whose increased aerobic respiration releases thermal energy—and contraction of **hair erector muscles** to trap an insulating layer of still air in furry mammals, plus permanent insulation by subcutaneous **fatty tissue**); why high Mumbai relative humidity slows sweat evaporation and raises heat-stroke risk when air temperature approaches or exceeds `37 °C`.
   - *Critical 0610 examiner trap (`:::careful`):* **Capillaries do NOT move** closer to or further from the skin surface, and capillary walls have no muscle layer to constrict or dilate; it is the muscular **arterioles** supplying the skin capillaries that **vasodilate** or **vasoconstrict**!
   - *Plates & Live Workbench:* `c10_skin_thermoregulation()` (`#fig-10-2`) + `:::live glucose_thermoregulation`, and `c10_mumbai_heat_balance()` (`#fig-10-3`).
   - *Safe Home Activity 1 (`:::home`):* Two thermometers or two identical steel tumblers/cups—one wrapped in a damp cotton handkerchief and one dry—measuring evaporative cooling in room air vs fan breeze (with explicit non-trialled educational design note).
3. **`10.3` The chemical cleanup crew: liver deamination, urea, and the kidney’s two-stage nephron ledger**
   - *Scene:* A protein-rich meal of *dal tadka* and *paneer* (or eggs) after a sweaty cricket match: your body can store spare glucose as glycogen and spare fat in adipose tissue, so why can't it store spare amino acids in a tank?
   - *Core concepts:* **Excretion** (removal of metabolic wastes, toxic materials, and substances in excess of requirements) vs **egestion** (`Chapter 1` & `Chapter 7`: undigested cellulose fibre in faeces never crossed the intestinal epithelium into cells); the three main excreted categories in `0610 Topic 13.1` (**carbon dioxide** from aerobic respiration excreted via the **lungs**; **urea**, **excess water**, and **excess mineral ions** excreted via the **kidneys** in urine; with a smaller variable loss of water, salts, and traces of urea in sweat); why excretion is essential (accumulated `CO₂` forms carbonic acid and lowers blood pH; accumulated nitrogenous waste is toxic to cells; unadjusted water/ion levels alter blood plasma water potential and damage cells by osmosis); **assimilation** of needed amino acids into proteins (`fibrinogen`, `albumin`, enzymes) in the **liver** vs **deamination** of excess amino acids (**removal of the nitrogen-containing amino part** of amino acids to form ammonia, which is immediately converted into less toxic **urea** `(NH₂)₂CO` and released into the **hepatic vein** to travel in blood plasma to the kidneys, while the remaining carbohydrate/carbon skeleton is respired or converted to glycogen); gross anatomy of the human urinary system (**renal artery**, **kidneys** with outer **cortex** and inner **medulla**, **renal vein**, **ureters**, **urinary bladder**, **sphincter muscle**, **urethra**); microscopic structure and two-stage mass-balance ledger of the **nephron** (~1 million per kidney):
     1. **Ultrafiltration at the glomerulus and renal (Bowman’s) capsule (in the cortex):** High hydrostatic pressure in the tangled **glomerulus** capillary knot (fed by a wider afferent arteriole and drained by a narrower efferent arteriole) forces **water, glucose, urea, and mineral ions** through the size-selective filtration barrier into the **renal capsule** (`~125 mL/min` or `~180 L/day` of glomerular filtrate in two adult kidneys), while **red blood cells, white blood cells, platelets, and large plasma proteins** are too large to pass and remain in the blood.
     2. **Selective reabsorption along the renal tubule and collecting duct (cortex and medulla):** Why filtering `180 L/day` without taking almost all of it back would dehydrate a human in under half an hour! In the **renal tubule** (especially the proximal convoluted tubule), **all (`100%`) of the glucose** (and amino acids) is selectively reabsorbed back into the surrounding peritubular blood capillaries by **active transport** (sodium-coupled carrier proteins powered by ATP in mitochondrion-rich tubule cells), along with **most of the water** (by osmosis through **Peter Agre’s aquaporins**, `:::story p-agre`) and **some mineral ions** (as needed to maintain plasma osmolarity). The remaining **urea**, **excess water**, and **excess ions** pass down the **collecting duct** into the renal pelvis and **ureter** as **urine** (`~1.5 L/day`, with urea concentrated ~60-fold from `0.03 g/100 mL` in plasma/filtrate to `~1.8–2.0 g/100 mL` in normal urine, while urine protein = `0` and urine glucose = `0`).
   - *Historical & Experimental Evidence:* **Carl Ludwig (1844)** physical filtration hypothesis vs **Joseph Wearn & Alfred Newton Richards (1924)** quartz-pipette micropuncture of living frog renal capsules proving directly that capsular fluid is a protein-free ultrafiltrate containing glucose (`Benedict's/reduction positive`) while bladder urine from the same kidney is glucose-free (`selective reabsorption` in the tubule).
   - *Plates & Live Workbench:* `c10_liver_excretion_urea()` (`#fig-10-4`), `c10_kidney_nephron_ledger()` (`#fig-10-5`) + `:::live nephron_filtration`.
   - *Safe Home Activity 2 (`:::home`):* Kitchen two-stage sieve and rescue model (*rajma* beans + fine *rava*/semolina + dissolved sugar + salt through a strainer/coffee filter, showing why a size sieve alone cannot separate two small dissolved molecules like glucose and urea—the kidney needs molecular carrier proteins to rescue 100% of the sugar afterwards; with explicit non-trialled disclosure).
4. **`10.4` The invisible border guard: pathogens, barriers, cholera, antibodies, and immunological memory**
   - *Scene:* The monsoon breaks over Mumbai in June: rainwater pools, crowded local trains, street-side *pani puri* water, and why your body is not overrun by microbes every week.
   - *Core concepts:* **Pathogen** (`a disease-causing organism`, plus non-living viruses) and **transmissible disease** (`a disease in which the pathogen can be passed from one host to another`); **direct contact** (through blood or other body fluids, skin-to-skin contact) vs **indirect transmission** (from contaminated surfaces, food, water, animals/vectors, or droplets in air); **first line of defence**: **mechanical barriers** (**skin** with tough keratinised outer epidermis + blood clotting by platelets converting soluble fibrinogen to insoluble fibrin mesh at cuts from Chapter 8; **hairs in the nose** filtering inhaled dust and droplets) and **chemical barriers** (**mucus** secreted by goblet cells in the trachea/bronchi and gut trapping pathogens before **ciliated epithelial cells** sweep mucus up to the throat; **stomach hydrochloric acid** providing low pH that kills many ingested bacteria; plus **lysozyme** enzyme in tears and saliva); **community hygiene and public-health barriers** (**clean water supply**, **hygienic food preparation** such as thorough cooking and refrigeration, **good personal hygiene** such as handwashing with soap, **waste disposal**, and **sewage treatment**); **Cholera (*Vibrio cholerae*) and Oral Rehydration Therapy (ORT)** (comma-shaped bacterium transmitted in water or food contaminated with faeces; **Supplement mechanism**: bacteria multiply in the small intestine and secrete **cholera toxin**, which binds to intestinal epithelial cells and causes active secretion of **chloride ions (`Cl⁻`)** into the lumen of the small intestine; this lowers the **water potential** of the gut lumen below that of the cells and blood, drawing massive volumes of **water out of cells and blood into the gut lumen by osmosis**, producing profuse watery **diarrhoea**— look how **Sambhu Nath De** in Kolkata (`1959`) proved a cell-free bacterial filtrate causes this fluid outpouring, and **Dilip Mahalanabis** (`1968–1971`, Bongaon refugee camp during the 1971 war) proved that **oral rehydration solution (ORS)**—clean water with a measured small amount of **salt (`NaCl`) and glucose**—saves lives because the intestinal `Na⁺`–glucose cotransporter stays intact and pulls `Na⁺`, glucose, and water back into the blood by osmosis); **second/third lines of defence (the immune system)**: **phagocytes** (non-specific **phagocytosis**: engulfing and digesting pathogens with hydrolytic enzymes in vacuoles) vs **lymphocytes** and **specific immune responses**: every pathogen carries surface **antigens** with a **specific 3D shape**; lymphocytes carrying matching surface receptors recognise the antigen, divide by mitosis (clonal selection/expansion), and secrete **antibodies**—proteins whose variable binding sites have a **complementary shape** to the antigen; how **antigen–antibody binding** destroys pathogens (either directly—clumping/agglutinating them, neutralising toxins, or lysing them—or by **marking/opsonising** them for rapid destruction by **phagocytes**); **active immunity** (`defence against a pathogen by antibody production in the body`, gained either after catching the infection or by **vaccination**, producing **memory cells** that remain in the blood/lymph for years or decades so a second encounter triggers a **much faster, much larger secondary antibody response** before symptoms develop); **how vaccination works** (introducing **weakened or dead pathogens or their antigens**—or mRNA/subunit antigens—to stimulate lymphocytes to make complementary antibodies and **memory cells**, plus **herd immunity** breaking transmission chains, illustrated with **Louis Pasteur** `:::story p-pasteur`, **Kamal Ranadive & C. V. Bapat** at the Indian Cancer Research Centre in Parel, Mumbai culturing the *ICRC bacillus* for leprosy vaccine work, and **Gagandeep Kang** at CMC Vellore demonstrating natural and **Rotavac** vaccine immunity against rotavirus in Indian infants); **passive immunity** (`short-term defence against a pathogen by antibodies acquired from another individual`, such as maternal antibodies crossing the **placenta** to the fetus or transferred in **breast milk / colostrum** to an infant, or injected antivenom/antitoxin: provides **immediate protection**, **no memory cells are produced**, and protection is **short-term** because the borrowed antibodies are gradually broken down as foreign/circulating proteins).
   - *Companion Story:* `:::story banting-mahalanabis-lifelines` (`source/src/stories/banting-mahalanabis-lifelines.md`) — pairing **Frederick Banting, Charles Best, J. J. R. Macleod, James Collip, and Leonard Thompson (Toronto, 1921–1922)** with **Sambhu Nath De (Kolkata, 1959) and Dilip Mahalanabis (Bongaon, 1971)**: two physiological rescues where understanding an exact molecular mechanism (pancreatic islet insulin vs intestinal `Na⁺`–glucose cotransport) turned a fatal metabolic or osmotic crisis into a life-saving treatment.
   - *Plates & Live Workbench:* `c10_pathogens_barriers_cholera()` (`#fig-10-6`), `c10_immune_response_vaccines()` (`#fig-10-7`) + `:::live immunity_vaccine_memory`, and `c10_active_vs_passive_immunity()` (`#fig-10-8`).
5. **Chapter-end Case (`:::case`): *The Sweet Urine and the Innocent Filter* (`#fig-10-9`: `c10_diabetes_renal_case()`)**
   - Unites **Section 10.1 (Pancreatic insulin & blood glucose)** and **Section 10.3 (Glomerular ultrafiltration & proximal tubular selective reabsorption)** in a diagnostic clinical reasoning case:
     - *Observations:* A 13-year-old patient presents with intense thirst (**polydipsia**), frequent large-volume urination (**polyuria**, `~3.8 L/day`), fatigue, and weight loss. Fasting blood plasma glucose is `16.0 mmol/L` (normal `4.0–5.5 mmol/L`). Urinalysis shows **high glucose in the urine (`glycosuria`)**, normal urea excretion, and **zero protein and zero red blood cells in the urine**.
     - *Three rival student hypotheses to evaluate and falsify/confirm:*
       1. *Student A:* “Glucose is in the urine because high blood pressure tore holes in the glomerulus and renal capsule filter.” → **Falsified:** Even in a healthy person, glucose (`M_r = 180`) is a tiny molecule that passes freely through the glomerular filter into the renal capsule (`filtrate glucose = plasma glucose`). Furthermore, if the glomerular size barrier were torn open, **large plasma proteins (albumin) and red blood cells** would leak into the urine—yet urine protein is **zero**.
       2. *Student B:* “The patient's kidney tubule cells have lost all their glucose carrier proteins or stopped making ATP.” → **Falsified:** The proximal tubule's sodium–glucose carrier proteins are healthy and working at full capacity, reabsorbing ~`10–11 mmol/L` worth of filtered glucose, **but** because untreated **Type 1 diabetes** (lack of **insulin** from the pancreas) has allowed plasma and filtrate glucose (`16.0 mmol/L`) to rise above the **renal transport maximum (`T_m ≈ 10–11 mmol/L`)**, every carrier protein in the proximal tubule is **saturated**; the excess filtered glucose cannot be reabsorbed and spills into the urine.
       3. *Student C:* “Why does spilling glucose into the tubule cause `3.8 L/day` of urine and fierce thirst?” → **Confirmed by osmosis (`Chapter 3`):** Unreabsorbed glucose remains dissolved in the renal tubule and collecting duct fluid, **lowering the water potential** of the tubular fluid. That osmotic pull opposes water reabsorption through the collecting duct's **aquaporins**, dragging extra water out into the urine (**osmotic diuresis / polyuria**), which dehydrates the blood plasma and triggers hypothalamic thirst receptors.

## 3. Primary Historical and Empirical Grounding Ledger

1. **Claude Bernard (1859/1865) & Walter B. Cannon (1926/1929) — *Milieu intérieur* and *Homeostasis*:**
   - **Claude Bernard** (*Leçons sur les propriétés physiologiques et les altérations pathologiques des liquides de l'organisme*, 1859; *Introduction à l'étude de la médecine expérimentale*, 1865) established two foundational insights used in Section `10.1`: (a) the liver stores carbohydrate as an insoluble reserve (**glycogen**, discovered by Bernard in 1855–1857) and releases glucose into hepatic venous blood between meals, and (b) Multicellular animals live not in the external air directly, but inside a liquid **internal environment (*milieu intérieur*)**—blood plasma and tissue fluid—whose constancy is the condition for free and independent life.
   - **Walter B. Cannon** (1926; *Organization for Physiological Homeostasis*, *Physiological Reviews* 1929; *The Wisdom of the Body*, 1932) coined **homeostasis** (from Greek *homoios*, similar, and *stasis*, standing still/condition)—explicitly noting that he chose *homoio-* ("similar, with room for small variation") rather than *homo-* ("rigidly identical") because living feedback systems oscillate slightly around a set point rather than freezing at a static number.
2. **Frederick Banting, Charles Best, J. J. R. Macleod, and James Collip (Toronto, 1921–1922) — Extraction of Insulin and Rescue of Leonard Thompson:**
   - Following Paul Langerhans (1869, describing pancreatic islet cell clusters) and Oskar Minkowski & Joseph von Mering (1889, showing pancreatectomy in dogs causes fatal diabetes mellitus with glycosuria), **Frederick Banting** and medical student **Charles Best** worked in **J. J. R. Macleod’s** laboratory at the University of Toronto in summer 1921 to extract the internal secretion of the pancreatic islets before trypsin could digest it. Biochemist **James Collip** purified the pancreatic extract so it could be safely injected into humans. On **11 January 1922** (first impure extract) and **23 January 1922** (Collip’s purified extract), 14-year-old **Leonard Thompson** at Toronto General Hospital—weighing ~29.5 kg (`65 lb`) and near death from Type 1 diabetes with severe hyperglycaemia and ketonuria/glycosuria—received the injections; his blood glucose fell from `520 mg/dL` (`~28.9 mmol/L`) to `120 mg/dL` (`~6.7 mmol/L`) within 24 hours, urinary glucose dropped from over `100 g/day` to near zero, and his strength returned (`Banting, Best, Collip, Campbell & Fletcher, CMAJ 1922`).
3. **Carl Ludwig (1844) & Joseph T. Wearn and Alfred Newton Richards (1924) — Micropuncture Proof of Glomerular Ultrafiltration and Selective Reabsorption:**
   - In 1844, **Carl Ludwig** proposed that the glomerulus acts as a physical pressure filter driven by arterial blood pressure, and that urine is concentrated as fluid passes down the renal tubule. For eighty years, vitalists argued instead that the capsule actively "secreted" urine until **Joseph T. Wearn and Alfred Newton Richards (1924)** (*Observations on the composition of glomerular urine, with particular reference to the problem of reabsorption in the renal tubules*, *American Journal of Physiology* 71:209–227) used a micromanipulator and a sharpened quartz capillary pipette (`10–20 µm` tip) under a stereoscopic microscope to withdraw micro-samples directly from **Bowman’s (renal) capsule** in living frogs. Their chemical tests proved directly that:
     - Fluid inside the renal capsule is **free of protein** (matching protein-free blood plasma ultrafiltrate),
     - Capsular fluid **contains dissolved glucose and chloride ions** at the same concentration as blood plasma, whereas
     - Bladder urine collected simultaneously from the ureter of the **very same kidney contains zero glucose** (`reabsorption of 100% of glucose` along the renal tubule).
4. **Peter Agre, Gregory Preston, William Guggino, and John Parker (1991–1992) — Aquaporin Water Channels (`:::story p-agre`):**
   - Already present in `source/src/stories/p-agre.md` and introduced in Chapter 3; explicitly linked in Section `10.3` to explain how the renal tubule and collecting duct achieve rapid, selective osmotic water reabsorption (`~178.5 L/day` out of `180 L/day` filtered) while keeping urea and ions from freely leaking through the water pore.
5. **Sambhu Nath De (Kolkata, 1959) & Dilip Mahalanabis (Bongaon, 1971) — Cholera Enterotoxin and Oral Rehydration Therapy (ORT):**
   - For over a century after **John Snow (1854)** mapped the Broad Street pump outbreak in London and **Robert Koch (1883–1884)** isolated comma-shaped *Vibrio cholerae* in Egypt and Kolkata, doctors assumed cholera killed by stripping away or perforating the intestinal wall. In **1959 at Nilratan Sircar Medical College, Calcutta (Kolkata)**, pathologist **Sambhu Nath De** (*Enterotoxicity of bacteria-free culture-filtrate of Vibrio cholerae*, *Nature* 183:1533–1534, 1959) injected a completely **bacteria-free sterile filtrate** of *V. cholerae* culture into ligated rabbit small-intestinal loops and showed that the mucosa remained structurally intact while fluid poured into the lumen—proving that a secreted **cholera exotoxin (enterotoxin)** drives fluid and electrolyte secretion across an intact epithelium.
   - Because the intestinal epithelium and its brush-border **`Na⁺`–glucose cotransporter (`SGLT1`, met in Chapter 7 Section `7.4`)** remain intact during cholera, clinical investigators in Dhaka and Kolkata (**David Nalin, Richard Cash, Norbert Hirschhorn, Nathaniel Pierce, and colleagues, 1968**) showed that drinking an oral solution containing both **sodium chloride and glucose** drives coupled `Na⁺` and glucose uptake into intestinal cells, raising intracellular and interstitial osmolarity and pulling water back into the blood by osmosis. During the **1971 Bangladesh Liberation War cholera epidemic** in the **Bongaon refugee camp** near the West Bengal border, paediatrician **Dilip Mahalanabis** (Johns Hopkins Center for Medical Research and Training, Calcutta) ran out of intravenous saline and empowered family members to administer simple packets of table salt, baking soda, potassium chloride, and glucose dissolved in clean boiled water (**ORS**) to over **3,700 severe cholera patients**, cutting the case-fatality rate from **~30% down to 3.6%** (*Mahalanabis et al., Johns Hopkins Medical Journal 1973*).
6. **Louis Pasteur (`:::story p-pasteur`), Kamal Ranadive & C. V. Bapat (Mumbai, 1958–1979), and Gagandeep Kang (Vellore, 2011–2014) — Vaccination and Immunological Memory:**
   - **Louis Pasteur** (`source/src/stories/p-pasteur.md`) is linked in Section `10.4` for germ theory and attenuated vaccines (fowl cholera, anthrax, rabies, 1879–1885).
   - **Kamal Ranadive (1917–2001)** and **C. V. Bapat** at the **Indian Cancer Research Centre (ICRC) in Parel, Mumbai** (just minutes from Dadar!) established India's first tissue-culture laboratory (`1950s`) and isolated and cultured a mycobacterial strain from lepromatous leprosy tissue (**the ICRC bacillus**, *Bapat, Ranadive & Khanolkar, International Journal of Leprosy 1958*), whose shared surface **antigens** laid the experimental foundation for an indigenous anti-leprosy vaccine tested in Maharashtra (`Deo, Bapat, Chulawala et al., 1981`).
   - **Gagandeep Kang** and colleagues at **Christian Medical College (CMC), Vellore** tracked birth cohorts of Indian children (*NEJM 2011*) and helped lead the clinical evaluation of India's indigenous oral rotavirus vaccine **Rotavac** (`116E`, *Bhandari, Rongsen-Chandola, Bavdekar, John, Antony, Taneja, Zaki, Kawade, Kang et al., The Lancet 2014*), showing how oral exposure to an attenuated viral antigen builds mucosal and systemic **memory lymphocytes** that prevent severe dehydrating rotavirus gastroenteritis in infants.
