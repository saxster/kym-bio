## Notes on biological models and numbers

Every diagram and interactive model in this book is a map, not the territory itself. A good map leaves out clutter so you can see how the mechanism works, and states plainly what it simplified:

1. **The crowded cytoplasm.** Textbook drawings (and our 2D and 3D cell cutaways) show a few organelles floating in open space so you can see each one clearly. In a real living cell, macromolecules occupy $20\text{–}30\%$ of the volume, packed as tightly as passengers in a rush-hour Dadar train, jostled millions of times a second by water molecules.
2. **Scale inside cell drawings.** In a real eukaryotic cell ($20\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ across), the cell membrane is only $7\text{–}8\text{ nm}$ thick and a ribosome is $25\text{ nm}$ wide—more than a thousand times narrower than the cell. To make membranes and ribosomes visible on the same screen as the nucleus, schematic cell diagrams draw them thicker than true scale. Whenever you want true logarithmic ratios, open <a href="#explore-scale">Powers of Ten in the Living World</a>.
3. **Overall equations vs. multi-step pathways.** Writing photosynthesis as $6\text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\text{O}_2$, or aerobic respiration as its reverse, is like summarising a thirty-station train journey by naming only Churchgate and Virar. The balanced equation strictly tracks the conservation of atoms and energy between start and finish, while the text shows the key intermediate stages (splitting water in thylakoids, the Calvin cycle, glycolysis, and rotary ATP synthase in mitochondria).
4. **Real measured numbers.** Cell counts ($\approx 3.0 \times 10^{13}$ human cells, Sender et al. 2016), ATP turnover, enzyme rates, Van Helmont's willow masses, and Mendel's pea ratios come from primary scientific literature and are documented in the curriculum and sources registry below.
